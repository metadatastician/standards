== 1. The request

The deed spec reads the estate and its repos as a choreography.
`estate_chora.deed` is the global side, each `<repo>_chora.deed` an endpoint
projection, and the one-declaration-site rule is the projection discipline.
Until 2026-11-18 the spec said its keystone, K-CUT, was an open conjecture,
and that no rule could depend on it. The owner ruled to update the fact and
lift the caution. A rule may now be *derived* from commuting projection.
This ADR says what such a derivation means for deeds and what it would cost.

== 3. The reading for deeds

The model has parties, a global type built from messages and binary
choices, a cut and a projection. Each is read as follows.

*Parties.* At a given frontier: the estate chora `E`, the registry
(chora-registry) `R`, each repo chora, each praxis deed's tool (the
cadastre, for example) and each operational tool. The set is finite at a
frontier (§7 says what that costs).

*Message* `s → r : g`. Party `r` takes a fact that `s` publishes, with grade
`g`. The flows the spec already draws in "The chora is not a layer" are
messages: `E → repo` (vocabulary, through `:beholding-chora`),
`repo → cadastre` (the cadastre reads every repo chora),
`cadastre → emission`, and `emission → operational tool`.

*Choice* `s → r chooses`. A decision taken by `s` and announced to `r`. In
the estate this is an owner ruling between alternatives, a registry switch
of classification scheme, or a praxis rule taking one branch. A party that
receives the announcement is a participant in the choice. Any other party
is outside it.

*Global type `G`.* The protocol of these reads and decisions over a bounded
window. This is a precision on the spec's sentence "`estate_chora.deed` is
the global type". `estate_chora.deed` declares the vocabulary in which the
messages of `G` are typed, and `G` is the flow. Today no file holds `G`.
Where it should live is question 2 (§11).

*Cut, or frontier.* How far the estate has run along each branch of `G`.
Concretely it is a set of pins, a deed at a commit for each party, as of
which the estate is read. `cutproj p c` is `p`'s own frontier: the pins that
`p`'s deed itself records.

*Projection.* `proj p G` is `p`'s local type: what `p` receives and
publishes, in order. A repo chora's local type is "receive the vocabulary
from `E`, publish facts to its readers". The grade restricted to `p`,
`grade↾ p`, counts only the messages `p` takes part in. A message `p` is
outside of contributes `𝟙` (upstream `at (outside _ _) _ = 𝟙`).

== 4. The resource algebra

K-1 holds for *any* algebra with `𝟙 ⊗ x ≡ x` and `x ⊕ x ≡ x` (K-4). `⊗`
sequences two steps on one timeline, and `⊕` combines the two branches of a
choice. Which algebra the estate uses decides what a deed's grade *means*.
That choice is the owner's (question 1). The candidates:

[cols="1,2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Candidate | What is graded | Why the two laws hold | Proof cost

| A. *Echo loss* (upstream `Loss`)
| Facts lost across a read: fields a reader cannot resolve, or that a
  mapping spec drops on purpose ("anything a mapping spec does not list
  does not translate"). `Grade = ℕ ∪ {∞}`, `⊗ = +` (loss accumulates, `∞`
  absorbs), `𝟙 = 0`, `⊕ = min`.
| `0 + x = x`; `min x x = x`.
| None. K-6 already discharges it at the resource-algebra pin. It matches
  the chapter's own Echo verdict ("each edge graded by an echo
  loss-grade"). One caution: `⊕ = min` reports the *less* lossy branch of a
  choice, which is optimistic for an audit.

| B. *Worst-case lag* (upstream `MaxPlus`)
| How far behind a party reads, counted in commits or pins along a chain
  of reads. `⊗ = +`, `𝟙 = 0`, `⊕ = max`.
| `0 + x = x`; `max x x = x`.
| None. K-6 discharges it. `⊕ = max` is pessimistic, which suits an audit.

| C. *Evidence strength, weakest link* (new)
| The status of what a fact rests on: `PROVEN ⊐ TESTED ⊐ ASSUMED ⊐ OPEN`.
  `⊗ = ⊕ = ⊓` (meet), `𝟙 = PROVEN`. This is the estate's existing rule
  that "a row is only as strong as the weakest step it depends on"
  (`docs/README-EXPLAINME-STANDARD.adoc`).
| `PROVEN ⊓ x = x`; `x ⊓ x = x`.
| A new instance in resource-algebra (Agda). Both laws are one line each,
  but the obligation is new and `OPEN` until checked.

| D. *Structural first, no grade*
| Nothing yet. Use K-2 and K-5, which need no algebra law, for the
  projection property, and choose a grade later.
| Not applicable.
| None. A product of any two of A–C is also lawful, componentwise, if a
  vector grade is wanted later (a new instance, `OPEN`).
|===

Recommendation, for the owner to rule: *A* is the most elegant, because it
needs no new proof and is the grade the chapter already names. *D* is the
most dependable, because it commits to the least. *C* gives the most useful
reading for an audit, at the price of a new obligation.

== 5. What becomes a stated property, and what rule follows

*The prose invariant, stated.* The spec says "an operational tool does not
read a source it is not a party to". With the reading of §3, this is: for
every party `p` and every frontier `c` consistent for `p`, `p`'s view up to
`c` is `proj p G ↾ₗ cutproj p c`. By K-2 that is the projection of the
estate cut at `c`. By K-1 the estate's account of `p` up to `c` equals the
account `p` reads from its own view. A source `p` is not party to
contributes `𝟙`, so it cannot change `p`'s account.

Two rules follow. Neither is adopted (question 4).

R1. *Locality of account.* A party's graded account up to a consistent
frontier is computed from its own deed and the pins it records, and from
nothing else. Consequence: chora-registry and the cadastre may compute each
repo's account one repo at a time, incrementally, and an edit to a deed a
repo does not read cannot change that repo's account. R1 is K-1 instantiated.
It adds no new mathematics.

R2. *No unannounced dependence.* If an estate decision changes what party
`p`'s deed must say, then `p` must take part in that decision: its deed
records the pin or the ruling that selected the branch. Otherwise the two
branches, cut at the frontier, must look the same to `p`. R2 is the side
condition `Consistent p c` (§6), turned into a rule a deed can be checked
against.

K-5 adds one more consequence: a deed is judged against the estate as of
its own pins. A later ruling, beyond the frontier, does not make an earlier
deed wrong. It only makes the deed's frontier old, which is the lag that
candidate B measures.

== 6. The side condition in deed terms

`Consistent p c` (upstream `Projection.agda`) holds when, at every choice
`p` is outside of, the two branches, truncated at `c`, project identically
for `p` (plain merge). Messages carry no condition.

In deed terms: when the owner, the registry or a tool decides something
and `p` is not told, `p`'s view up to the frontier must not depend on which
way the decision went.

*What fails without it.* K-7 shows that the equality breaks: the estate's
account of `p` and `p`'s own account differ (upstream's fixtures give 1
against 2). In the estate this is *silent drift*. For example, a template
change in rsr-template-repo or scaffoldia alters what a repo is expected to
contain, under a decision the repo never received. The registry's account
of that repo and the repo's own account then disagree, and neither is
wrong by its own lights. R2 makes that failure visible: the repo either
records the selection it received, or it must be indifferent to it.

Plain merge is stricter than full merge, so R2 is conservative. It may
refuse a dependence that full merge would accept (§7).

== 7. Synchronous or asynchronous, and what is outside both

Deeds are read *asynchronously*. Publishing a commit and a reader pinning
it are separate events, and between them the fact is in flight. Y-1
(upstream) shows that synchronous cuts are exactly the asynchronous cuts
with nothing in flight. So K-1 covers only settled snapshots, where every
reader has caught up. The estate is never settled, because `main` moves, so
this ADR recommends stating R1 and R2 over Y-2 (question 3).

The transport side of K-CUT is *not* relied on. It is proved upstream for
this fragment and points to a later rule. By Y-5, Y-6 and Y-8, the estate
does not know that a repo has adopted a ruling until the repo's deed cites
it back ("acknowledgement by pin"). That is noted as a future proposal, not
made here.

Outside both K-1 and Y-2:

* *Recursion.* Repeated reads, such as the cadastre's periodic run, and
  mutual reads (A reads B, B reads A) are recursive protocols. Only a
  finite window, unrolled, is covered. A claim about all time is not.
* *Delegation.* A repo transfer, a rename (the id is unchanged under U1,
  but the party's role passes), a fork that takes over a role, and a tool
  handing work to another (gitbot-fleet dispatch) are all outside.
* *Party creation.* `G` has a fixed set of parties. A repo born after the
  frontier is a new `G`.
* *Full merge, queues as data, data-dependent warrants.* Outside, as
  upstream states.

== 9. What changes in `deed.abnf` and `deed_lint`

Nothing, at first. R1 and R2 are semantics. If `G` is derived from fields
that deeds already carry (`:beholding-chora`, praxis rules, routing) and
from pins, no production changes, and a later `deed_lint` mode could
report R2 as an advisory finding. If `G` is to be *declared* (question 2),
that is a new clause and a grammar change, and it needs a separate ADR.

== 10. Alternatives, compatibility, tensions

*Alternatives.* (a) Keep the vocabulary and derive no rule, which was the
spec's position before 2026-11-18. (b) Derive rules from the synchronous
K-1 only, on settled snapshots. (c) Wait for PWG-2ii (both recheck legs)
before deriving anything. The owner's ruling to lift the caution rules
out (a) as the end state, but leaves (b) and (c) open as sequencing.

*Compatibility.* No existing deed, grammar or validator changes. R1 and R2,
if adopted, start as advisory findings and never block (the `:review`
strategy).

*Tensions.*

* The rule rests on a theorem that is ungraded. That is allowed by the
  owner's ruling, but a claim that the rule is "machine-checked" would need
  receipts (PROOF-POLICY §8).
* `estate_chora.deed` "is the global type" is loose (§3). Ruling on
  question 2 settles it.
* Under candidate A, `⊕ = min` reads optimistically at choices (§4).
* chora-registry is authoritative for estate-wide placement. R1 fits that:
  the registry computes, and a repo's copy is checked against it.
