Specification A4 of the Estate Reconciliation Plan. The office decides and the farm executes (R2). A4 defines the line between them, the one envelope in which every finding and every verdict enters the office, the work order that leaves it, and the rules for moving the farm's label and merge scripts.

== 1. Summary

* The office decides: desired state, repo lifecycle, the intake ledger and the work orders. It reads the generated index (A1) from chora-registry, where it lives (Q3). The farm hosts execution: credentials, runners, scheduling and the actuator switch. The farm decides nothing about what to change (R2 [O]).
* One executor path: office work order, then evaluator, then Kin gate, then `robot-repo-automaton` running on the farm, then rescan, then outcome [O]. Fleet bots are sensors. A bot reports a SARIF row and a report-back, and writes nothing.
* One envelope for findings, check verdicts, gate verdicts and outcomes: a SARIF 2.1.0 body inside an in-toto Statement, signed in a DSSE envelope [O]. It resolves A3 E9.
* A work order is a deed clause profile `(work-order ...)` in a `praxis-deed`. A k9 contract can be generated from it later [O].
* The ledger holds fingerprints, not findings. A finding reaches a repo as one change or none: at most one open order per repo and fix class.
* The actuator is in `dry-run` by default. It moves to `canary` and then to `armed` only after the preconditions of section 8.3. No order class may use a ruleset bypass.
* The move of the farm's label and merge scripts is governed by rules (section 9). A4 does not move any script.

== 3. The boundary

=== 3.1 Who owns what

[cols="2,2,3"]
|===
| Concern | Owner | Note

| Repo lifecycle: birth, enrolment, life, death | Office | Phase 1 is dispatch-only (section 7).
| The generated index (A1 section 8) | chora-registry | `ATLAS.deed` is the finding index and lives in chora-registry (Q3 [O], 2026-11-14). The generator runs on the farm and commits its output there. The office reads it and does not hold a copy.
| The intake ledger | Office | Section 6.
| Work orders | Office | Section 7.
| Evaluation of an order | Fleet (evaluator) | Wave 1 [M]. Not built.
| Admissibility of an execution | Kin gate | Wave 1 and 5 [M].
| The executor program | `robot-repo-automaton` | The one executor [O].
| Hosting the executor: credentials, runners, scheduling, the actuator switch | Farm [O] | The farm never chooses a target or a change.
| Sensing | Fleet bots, `pons`, `panic-attack`, Hypatia, `cadastra` | They report. They do not write to repos.
| Mirrors | Farm | A mirror is an execution of a decided state. The decision is in the repo chora `(forges)` and the platform choras (A1).
| The post-merge verification of the gate (A3 section 9.3) | `cadastra` mode, run by the farm | Section 10.
|===

=== 3.2 The three rules of the line

. *The farm holds secrets and the office holds none.* Credentials are named by role in an order. The farm resolves a role to a credential. An order, an envelope and a ledger entry never carry a secret.
. *No script both decides and acts.* A component that picks targets or changes does not call an API with a write credential. A component that calls a write API takes its targets and its change from an order.
. *Execution is replayable from the order.* Given the order and the base commit, a dry run produces the same action list as an armed run. The outcome records both.

=== 3.3 What the farm may not do

The farm may not: create a repo unattended (D34(E) [O]); use a ruleset bypass; edit a licence or an SPDX header of existing content (`standards` rule [M]); run an order whose class is not in the class table; run an order past its expiry; run an order while the actuator is not armed.

== 5. The envelope

=== 5.1 One envelope for four things

[cols="2,3"]
|===
| `predicateType` | Carries

| `urn:estate:finding-set:v1` | The findings of one sensor run on one commit.
| `urn:estate:check-verdict:v1` | The result of one A3 check on one commit (`passed`, `failed`, `not_run`, `not_applicable`).
| `urn:estate:gate-verdict:v1` | The `estate-gate` verdict of A3: required set, states, waivers, pins.
| `urn:estate:outcome:v1` | The outcome of one work order (section 8.4).
|===

The `urn:` type names are my choice (decision F1). In-toto requires a URI for `predicateType` [I].

=== 5.2 Layers

----
DSSE envelope        payloadType "application/vnd.in-toto+json"; signatures[]
  in-toto Statement  subject[] (the repo at a commit); predicateType; predicate
    predicate        producer, status, and the body (SARIF 2.1.0 for a finding-set)
----

* The Statement is `https://in-toto.io/Statement/v1`. Its `subject` is one entry: `name` is the repo UUID (A1 `:repo-uuid` value as a string) and `digest` is `{"gitCommit": "<40 hex>"}` [I: in-toto accepts `gitCommit` as a digest key].
** *Resolved: ADR-008.* Under owner ruling V8 (A1, 2026-11-12) the repo UUID is the ADR-008 profile C (v8) id of the birth path, so the bare value in `subject.name` passes the estate UUID gate (`scripts/check-uuid-v8.sh`) as written, and `examples/a4-statement.json.example` needs no `.uuid-v8-exempt` entry [O].
* The signed payload is the Statement canonicalised with JCS (RFC 8785) [I]. The estate routes I-JSON/JCS data through `kyq` [M], so the same canonical form serves the signature and the data route.
* A signature has one of 2 forms, matching the 2 publisher forms of section 12.
** *ed25519.* The signature has a `keyid` and an ed25519 signature [I: DSSE]. The `keyid` is a publisher id in the federation root's `(publishers)` clause (A3 E1) whose entry carries `:ed25519`. A signature whose `keyid` is not listed is not valid.
** *OIDC.* The signature is a Sigstore bundle over the same DSSE payload [I: GitHub artifact attestations]. It is valid only when: the bundle verifies; the certificate's OIDC issuer equals the `:oidc-issuer` of a listed publisher; the certificate's workflow identity (the reusable workflow path and ref) equals that publisher's `:workflow` at the 40-hex SHA that A3 pins; and the certificate's source commit equals the Statement's subject commit. A workflow at any other path or SHA is not that publisher.
** *OIDC, `evaluate` mode on a pull request.* A `pull_request` run executes at GitHub's synthetic merge commit, not at the PR head that is its subject [I: GitHub `pull_request` event documentation]. For a `gate-verdict` with `mode: evaluate` whose predicate records `executedAt`, the certificate's source commit equals `executedAt` instead, and `executedAt` must be a merge commit whose parents include the Statement's subject commit. A `push` or `merge_group` run executes at its subject, so it records no `executedAt` and keeps the rule above.
** *OIDC, `publish` mode.* A `gate-verdict` with `mode: publish` (5.5) is signed by the A3 publisher (A3 4.4), a `workflow_run` listener. GitHub runs it at the last commit of the default branch, not at the head it judges [I: GitHub `workflow_run` documentation], so its certificate's source commit is the default branch's. For this mode the rule above is replaced by three conditions. The certificate's source commit is a commit on the subject repo's default branch. The certificate's workflow identity is the publisher's `:workflow` at the pinned SHA. When the verdict is `pass`, the predicate's `basis` is `accepted` and names, by sha256, an `evaluate` envelope whose subject equals this Statement's subject and which passes A3 4.4's acceptance rule. A `pending` or `failure` verdict may name a basis that is `in_progress`, `missing` or `rejected`. The subject head is therefore bound by the `basis`, and the signer by its certificate; neither stands in for the other. Every other predicate keeps the rule above. A4 does not fix the issuer value; the owner records it in the root.

Samples: `docs/estate-reconciliation/examples/a4-statement.json.example` and `docs/estate-reconciliation/examples/a4-envelope.json.example` parse as JSON [M]. I did not check them against the in-toto or DSSE schemas [I].

=== 5.3 `finding-set` predicate

[cols="2,1,4"]
|===
| Field | Required | Rule

| `producer` | yes | `{id, version}`: the sensor and its version or commit.
| `status` | yes | `ran`, `not_run`, `error` or `not_applicable`. `reason` is required unless `ran` [M: same rule as the report-back envelope].
| `scope` | yes | What was scanned: `{ref, paths}`. Paths are repo-relative with no `./`.
| `sarif` | when `ran` | A SARIF 2.1.0 log with exactly one run for one tool. It is a log; a log of another version is rejected.
|===

A `finding-set` with `status` other than `ran` carries no `sarif` and is itself a datum: it states that nothing was checked. A consumer MUST NOT treat it as clean (the pass-on-absence defect [M: A3 finding 6]).

Estate fields in each SARIF `result`:

[cols="2,4"]
|===
| Where | Content

| `ruleId` | The rule identifier. A5 section 3 fixes the form `<domain>/<name>`. A sensor's own id maps by A5 section 3.3 (for example `pons/<rule id>`).
| `properties.estate.severity` | `critical`, `high`, `medium`, `minor` (the estate's four columns [M: verdict grid]). The producer's own severity stays in the SARIF `level` and in `properties.original`.
| `properties.estate.evidenceClass` | The producer's evidence class (for `pons`, the tier and evidence class, so a heuristic is never read as a proof [M: verdict grid]).
| `partialFingerprints["estateFingerprint/v1"]` | The ledger fingerprint (section 6.2).
| `locations[0].physicalLocation.artifactLocation.uri` | Repo-relative path.
|===

=== 5.4 Mapping from the envelopes that exist

A sensor that cannot emit SARIF directly is wrapped by an adapter. The adapter is specified here and is not built:

[cols="2,3"]
|===
| Existing envelope | Mapping

| Report-back envelope (fleet member) | `reporter` to `producer.id`; `status` and `reason` unchanged; `ref` to `producer.version`; each `findings[]` entry to a SARIF `result` (`rule_id` to `ruleId`; `severity` through the estate normalisation of the verdict grid; `file` and `line` to the location).
| `panic-attack-findings.json` | `status`, `ref`, `reason` as above; each `report.weak_points[]` to a `result` (`category` to `ruleId`).
| `pons-findings.json` | each `report.findings[]` to a `result` (`rule_id`, `tier`, `evidence`, `location`).

| A3 owned-compute baton (`BAG-CHECK-BATON v2`) | `check_id` to the `check-verdict` check id; `verdict` and `exit_code` to the state; `node` to `producer.id`. |===

=== 5.5 `check-verdict` and `gate-verdict` predicates

* `check-verdict`: `check` (the A3 capability id), `state` (`passed`, `failed`, `not_run`, `not_applicable`), `exit_code`, `reason`, `producer`.
* `gate-verdict`: the fields A3 section 3.4 lists: head SHA, pin and sha256 of the aggregate and of the check table, blob SHA of the repo chora, the required set with each state, the waivers applied, the publisher id and the time. Also:
** `mode`: `evaluate` for the envelope the caller's run writes, or `publish` for the one the A3 publisher signs and posts (A3 4.4). Required.
** `inlineInputs`: one entry per inline-job input of A3 5.3, `{check, workflowPath, event, runId, runAttempt, conclusion}`, the attempt the verdict was computed from. Required in both modes; empty only when the table has no inline-job rows. The publisher compares it with the latest attempts before it posts.
** `basis`: for `mode: publish`. It is `{state, runId, runAttempt}`. `state` is `accepted`, `in_progress`, `missing` or `rejected`. With `accepted` it also carries `sha256`, the digest of the `evaluate` envelope the verdict is based on. With `rejected` it carries `reason`. Absent for `mode: evaluate`.
** `executedAt`: for `mode: evaluate` from a `pull_request` run, the 40-hex synthetic merge commit the run executed at (5.2). Absent otherwise.

The status posted to GitHub (A3 section 3.3) has `target_url` pointing to the DSSE envelope of the `gate-verdict`. Storage is in section 5.7.

=== 5.6 Validity rules

An envelope is *valid* when: the DSSE signature verifies for a listed publisher, by the rule of 5.2 for that publisher's form (ed25519 or OIDC, never one for the other); the payload parses as the Statement; the subject has exactly one entry, whose `name` equals the `:repo-uuid` of the repo being checked; the subject digest names a commit that exists in that repo; the predicate matches its type; and the SARIF log, if present, parses as 2.1.0. A `gate-verdict` is also invalid when `mode` or `inlineInputs` is missing; when `mode` is `publish` and `basis` is missing; when its verdict is `pass` and `basis.state` is not `accepted`; when `basis.state` is `accepted` and the envelope it names has another subject; or when `mode` is `evaluate` and `basis` is present. A valid envelope is not a *true* one. It says the publisher signed these bytes. Truth is checked by the observer (section 10).

=== 5.7 Storage

* An envelope is evidence about one commit and is never committed to the repo that it concerns [M: the verdict grid and the lane spec both say so].
* A finding-set, check-verdict and outcome envelope is stored by the office in an envelope store outside git history of any estate repo. Until a store exists, the farm keeps them in a directory the office reads, addressed by sha256 of the envelope. The office repo holds the ledger (section 6), not the envelopes.
* A gate-verdict envelope is reachable from the status `target_url`. The retention of envelopes is the owner's decision (decision F14). A3 requires that the envelope of a merged head be kept for as long as the observer may verify it.
* VeriSimDB is the planned store [M: Wave 4] and is not deployed [R]. The store's API is behind the interface of this section so that it can replace the directory later.

== 6. The ledger

=== 6.1 What it holds

The ledger holds one record per fingerprint. It does not hold findings or envelopes. A finding is evidence about one commit and ages out. The ledger tracks the state of a problem across commits.

The ledger is JSON Lines, I-JSON, in the office repo, one file per repo UUID: `ledger/<repo-uuid-hash>.jsonl` [I: my layout]. One record per line:

[cols="2,4"]
|===
| Field | Meaning

| `fp` | The fingerprint (6.2).
| `repo` | The repo UUID.
| `rule` | The rule identifier.
| `first_seen`, `last_seen` | Commit SHA and date.
| `sensors` | Sorted list of producer ids that reported it. Its length is the corroboration count.
| `state` | `open`, `ordered`, `fixed`, `accepted`, `lapsed`.
| `order` | The open work order id, when `state` is `ordered`.
| `accepted_by` | The deviation `:id` (A2), when `state` is `accepted`.
|===

States: `open` is a finding that nothing is acting on. `ordered` has an open work order. `fixed` means a later rescan on a later commit, whose `scope` (5.3) covered the finding's `path`, no longer reports it. A rescan whose scope did not cover the path leaves the state unchanged. `accepted` means a valid deviation covers it (A2 section 7). `lapsed` means the deviation that accepted it expired. The state is derived by the office from observed rescans and from the deviations at the head. It is never set by a sensor or by a repo's own statement (the self-assertion rule [M]).

=== 6.2 Fingerprint

----
fp = "sha256:" + hex(SHA-256( "estate-fp-v1" 0x00 rule 0x00 repo-uuid 0x00 path 0x00 anchor 0x00 occurrence ))
----

* `rule` is the `ruleId`. `repo-uuid` is the A1 `:repo-uuid` value. `path` is repo-relative.
* `anchor` is the SHA-256 of the normalised text of the line at the location (whitespace collapsed, line ending stripped), or the empty string for a finding with no line. A finding that moves by an unrelated edit keeps its fingerprint. A finding whose line changes gets a new one, and the old one goes `fixed` at the next rescan.
* `occurrence` is the decimal ordinal, from 1 in file order, of this finding among the findings in the file with the same `rule` and `anchor`. Two findings of one rule on 2 identical lines therefore get 2 fingerprints. An unrelated edit does not change the ordinal; inserting or deleting another line with the same text before it does.
* A sensor that has no line text uses its own anchor field and records its recipe in `producer`. The recipe is part of the fingerprint's meaning, and a change to it is a change of `estate-fp-v1`.

=== 6.3 Deduplication and aggregation

. The same fingerprint from two sensors is one record, with both in `sensors`. It is not two findings (the plan's "lose their duplicates" [R]).
. At most one open work order exists for a pair `(repo, class)`. A new finding of that class on that repo joins the open order and does not create a second one.
. A repo receives at most one change per fix class per cycle. Where several classes are ready for one repo, the office emits one order per class, and the executor MAY combine their branches into one draft PR only if the orders list each other as `:batch-with`. A4 does not define batching beyond that field.
. A decision that applies to all repos (a dependency update, a rule change) is made once in the office, as one canonical decision, and fans out as per-repo orders [R: plan, "occurs once, in the 1 home"].

== 7. Work orders

=== 7.1 Form

A work order is a `praxis-deed` named `<order-id>_praxis.deed` with a `(work-order)` clause profile. It uses the head that A2 chose for packs and A3 chose for the check table. It needs no grammar change.

----
(praxis-deed
  :schema-version "1.0.0"
  :canonical-name "wo-0001"
  :beholding-chora #uuid"0889b37b-defb-831f-973c-e119500db00e"
  (work-order
    :id "wo-0001"
    :class "fix-unpinned-actions"
    :repo #uuid"806d4d6f-f107-8b36-939b-f89ba6279377"
    :base "<40 hex>"
    :tier review
    :trigger dispatch
    :authority "owner/standards#1234"
    :expires "2026-12-31"
    (findings (finding :fingerprint "sha256:..."))
    (limits :max-files 10 :max-lines 200)
    (acceptance
      (require :check "secret-scan" :state passed)
      (require :rescan "fix-unpinned-actions" :findings 0))
    (escalate :when "any-test-failure" :to owner)))
----

The sample is `docs/estate-reconciliation/examples/a4-wo-0001_praxis.deed.example`. It lints clean under `deed_lint.js` as written: `deed.abnf` v1.3.0 and its linter accept the opaque literal since #1473 [M: linted 2026-11-12]. The linter checks syntax only [I].

=== 7.2 Fields

[cols="2,1,4"]
|===
| Field | Cardinality | Rule

| `:id` | 1, STRING | Unique in the office. Equals `:canonical-name`.
| `:class` | 1, STRING | A class in the class table (7.4).
| `:repo` | 1, UUID | The repo UUID (A1). Never a name.
| `:base` | 1, STRING | 40 lowercase hex: the commit the order was computed on. An executor refuses an order whose base is not an ancestor of the default branch head.
| `:tier` | 1, symbol | `report` or `review` (7.3).
| `:trigger` | 1, symbol | `dispatch` (a person or a labelled event starts it) or `schedule`. Lifecycle classes allow only `dispatch` (7.5).
| `:authority` | 1, STRING | A ruling or an issue, as in A2. An order with no authority is not valid.
| `:expires` | 1, STRING | `YYYY-MM-DD`. An executor refuses an expired order.
| `(findings)` | 1 | One or more `finding :fingerprint`. Every fingerprint is in the ledger, in state `open` or `ordered`.
| `(limits)` | 1 | `:max-files` and `:max-lines`, integers. An executor stops and escalates when a change exceeds them.
| `(acceptance)` | 1 | One or more `require`. `:check` plus `:state` names an A3 check state that must hold on the result. `:rescan` plus `:findings N` requires the rescan of that class to report at most N.
| `(escalate)` | 1 or more | `:when` STRING condition id, `:to` symbol (`owner`).
| `:batch-with` | 0..1, list of STRING | Other order ids that may share one PR.
|===

An order contains no secret, no command and no free-form script. The steps are the executor's recipe for the class (7.4). This follows the rule that no executable policy hides in a string [M: same rule as the mother contract].

=== 7.3 Tiers

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Tier | Meaning

| `report` | The executor computes the change and writes no branch. The outcome records the diff.
| `review` | The executor pushes a branch and opens a draft PR with the `pr-author` role. It does not merge [M: Wave 1, `review` tier].
|===

No `direct` tier exists. Adding one needs an owner ruling and a new role.

=== 7.4 Class table

Classes are data in the office. A class has: an id, the sensor rule ids it answers, the executor recipe (a `robot-repo-automaton` fixer or a leaf it calls), the credential role, the maximum tier, and a canary repo. The first population is provisional and reproduces what the estate already planned:

[cols="2,2,2"]
|===
| Class | Max tier | Role

| `fix-workflow-timeout-minutes`, `fix-workflow-permissions`, `fix-unpinned-actions`, `fix-stale-template-placeholders`, `fix-tracked-package-lock`, `fix-gitattributes-eol`, `fix-dependabot-coverage`, `fix-http-to-https`, `fix-tmp-paths`, `fix-shell-quoting` (the 10 [M]) | `review` | `pr-author`
| `repin-caller` (A3 section 4.2, rule 6) | `review` | `pr-author`
| `apply-ruleset` (A3 G01) | `report` until the applier exists | `applier`
|===

Classes for label and merge actions are added only by the move in section 9, each with an owner-approved tier. A class that edits a licence file, an SPDX header or a licence text is not allowed. The class table validator rejects it. [M: standards rule]

=== 7.5 Lifecycle orders

Birth, enrolment and retirement of a repo are classes whose only allowed `:trigger` is `dispatch`. No order creates a repo unattended [O: D34(E) as inherited]. Enrolment means: the repo has a chora (A1), its estate names it, and the next index run lists it. Retirement records the state in `(lineage)` and in `STATE.deed` (A7 J15; `(status)` no longer lives in the chora). A4 does not define the creation procedure: that is #636's phase 1, which is blocked on the owner.

== 8. The executor path

=== 8.1 Stages

----
office: finding -> ledger -> order
  evaluator      is the order admissible? (fleet; Wave 1; not built)
  Kin gate       may this execution run? (Wave 1 and 5; wiring not verified)
  executor       robot-repo-automaton, hosted on the farm, with the class's role
  rescan         the sensors that raised the findings run again on the result
  outcome        signed outcome envelope; ledger updated by the office
----

Each stage can refuse. A refusal is an `outcome` with the stage name and a reason. A refusal is not an error and is never retried without a new order.

=== 8.2 Actuator states

The actuator has 4 states: `off`, `dry-run`, `canary` and `armed`. The default is `dry-run` [R: plan P7]. `off` and `dry-run` write nothing to a repo. A `dry-run` produces the action list and records it in the outcome. `canary` writes only to the canary repos that the owner names when authorising it, and refuses every other repo. `armed` may write to any repo the class applies to.

=== 8.3 Preconditions for `armed`

The actuator MAY be armed for a class only when all 4 hold:

. The evaluator exists and has evaluated the order (Wave 1 [M]: "nothing leaves exit 78 until the evaluator exists").
. The Kin gate is on the path between the evaluator and the executor, and a refusal test shows it refusing (the ultraplan records that its safety processes are called by no dispatch path today [M]).
. The credential for the class's role exists and is held by the farm. For `applier` this is O11 [R].
. A canary repo has passed a `dry-run` and a `canary` run of the class with the outcome verified by a rescan.

The move from `dry-run` to `canary` is a separate owner authorisation per class. It needs preconditions 1 to 3 and names the canary repos. It is the only way to run the first write.

Arming is an owner action, per class. The `canary` and `armed` states are recorded in the office. The farm refuses a write for a class that is not marked `armed` there, except a write to a named canary repo of a class marked `canary`.

=== 8.4 Outcome

The `outcome` predicate carries: the order id; the executor and version; the actuator state; the action list (each action with the base and result commit, or a diff digest); the stage that ended the run; the rescan references; the resulting state of each ledger fingerprint; and the credential role used. It never carries a secret. The farm signs it. The office reads it and updates the ledger. The office never trusts an outcome's own claim that a finding is fixed: `fixed` is set from a later rescan (6.1).

=== 8.4.1 No bypass

No class, role or executor path may use a ruleset bypass, an `--admin` merge, a force push, or a merge of a PR whose required `estate-gate` is not `success`. A4 test X06 checks the class table and the role definitions for these.

== 9. The move of the label and merge scripts

R2 moves the farm's label and merge scripts to the office [R: plan, R2]. A4 gives the rules. It does not give the table.

=== 9.1 Classification

Every script gets exactly one class:

[cols="1,4"]
|===
| Class | Rule

| `D` (decide) | It chooses targets, labels, merge candidates or timing. It moves to the office as an order emitter or a policy over the ledger. It keeps no write credential.
| `X` (execute) | It calls a write API with a credential. It stays in the farm as a leaf that the executor calls, with its targets and arguments taken from an order. It decides nothing.
| `S` (split) | It does both. It is split into one `D` part and one `X` part before it moves. A script that cannot be split is `U`.
| `M` (mirror) | It mirrors a decided state to a forge. It stays in the farm.
| `U` (unclassified) | The owner decides. It does not move and does not run armed.
|===

=== 9.2 Procedure

. One script at a time. One PR per script, in the office or the farm repo that it touches. No bulk move and no sweep [M: the estate's standing rule].
. For each `D` or `S` script: write its order emitter and its class (7.4) in the office, with a tier no higher than the script's current effect. Merge scripts get no class above `review`; a merge class needs an owner ruling and the `merger` role.
. Parity test: run the old script and the new emitter plus executor in `dry-run` on a fixed repo snapshot. The action lists MUST be equal as sets. A difference is reported and the script does not retire.
. Retire the old script only after the parity test passes and the class is armed for a canary. Until then the old script stays and its actuator state is `dry-run` or `off`.
. Record the move in the office's class table: the class, the old script's name, and the parity evidence.

=== 9.3 The table is outside the repos

The per-script table (name, class, split plan, parity result) is a private working document. It stays outside any repo, in a private page. It is kept by whoever can read the farm. The spec, the class table and every commit name a class and never an unmoved private script. This keeps the private audit out of repos [O].

== 10. The observer

A3 section 9.3 requires an observer that verifies every merged head. It is `cadastra`, in a verify mode, run by the farm on a schedule [I: `cadastra` is the estate's observer [M]; the mode does not exist].

For each merged head the observer: reads the `estate-gate` status and its `target_url`; fetches the envelope; checks validity (5.6, by the signature form of its publisher); recomputes the verdict from the recorded inputs; compares it with the posted status; and checks the status creator against the publisher list. It emits a `finding-set` with rule `gate/forged-or-unverified`, `gate/unsigned`, `gate/mismatch` or `gate/no-envelope`. That finding enters the ledger like any other. The observer writes no status and changes no repo.

== 11. Relation to the other specifications

* A1: `(publishers)` in the root signs every envelope. The repo and estate UUIDs address every record.
* A2: `(deviations)` is what moves a ledger record to `accepted`. `(generation)` pins the generator whose output the rescan reads.
* A3: `check-verdict` and `gate-verdict` are the verdict forms of section 5. The caller's evaluation run signs its `evaluate` envelope where GitHub grants it a token, and the A3 publisher signs the `publish` envelope it posts, both as section 12 (F3) says. Owned-compute inputs are `check-verdict` envelopes.
* A5: owns the rule identifier namespace, the rule registries and the generated ignore files. A4 uses the ids of A5 section 3.
* A6: owns the home of the ruleset and settings canon that the office applies.

== 12. Signing in Actions

A3 section 9.1 left the key of the Actions aggregate to A4. The options are:

[cols="2,3"]
|===
| Option | Effect

| OIDC-bound attestation (proposed, F3) | The aggregate signs through GitHub's workflow identity. A verifier checks that the signing identity is the pinned reusable workflow at the pinned SHA, and needs no secret in the repo [I: GitHub artifact attestations and Sigstore]. The publisher entry in the root gets the OIDC form of A1 (`:oidc-issuer` and `:workflow`, with no `:kind` field), which names the issuer and the pinned workflow path.
| Estate-wide signing secret | A secret available to every caller's Actions run. Any workflow in a repo could read it and sign anything. Rejected.
| Unsigned | The observer reports `gate/unsigned` for every Actions-published head. Safe but noisy, and it leaves no way to tell a real aggregate from a forgery.
|===

A4 proposes the first (F3). It is an amendment to A1's `(publishers)`: an entry is either `(publisher :id :ed25519 ...)` or `(publisher :id :oidc-issuer ... :workflow ...)`. I did not test any attestation tooling [I].
