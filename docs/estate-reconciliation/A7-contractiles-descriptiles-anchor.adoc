Specification A7 of the Estate Reconciliation Plan. It defines how a repository's edicts (the contractiles) are checked against its identity (the chora) and its audited reality (the descriptiles), how the anchor states a repo's semantic authority and golden path, and the order in which the estate leaves A2ML behind.

== 1. Summary

* *Three layers* [O: W1]. The *chora* says what the repo is (A1). The *descriptiles* say what it is like now, from audit. The *contractiles* say what must hold, given what it is. No edict hangs off the clade. Its placements are identity facts in the chora, saying where the repo sits [O: W10, amending A1 R4]. Estate-wide classification schemes live in chora-registry, which is authoritative. A repo's own content organisation is `CLADE.deed`.
* *An edict is not a checklist* [O: W2]. Each contractile rule has two halves: `applies-when`, a test on identity and context, and `requires`, a test on reality. It is violated when the first holds and the second does not. A list of ticks is a descriptile. This is the aspect-oriented shape: identity selects the rules that apply.
* *Files, and the case of their names* [O: W3, W4]. Contractiles are Capitalised deeds, one per verb (`Mustfile.deed`, `Trustfile.deed` …), each checked by its K9 runner `<verb>.k9.ncl`. Descriptiles are ALL-CAPS deeds (`META.deed`, `STATE.deed` …). The chora deed stays the one identity record of the repo (D312). The case tells a reader which side a file is on, so nobody "fixes" an edict to match how things turned out.
* *Keeping a state you like means redeclaring the edict* [O: W5]. A descriptile edit never changes policy.
* *The anchor is a clause of the chora deed* [O: Q11, S1]. `(anchor …)` states the repo's semantic authority (what a downstream repo may extend but not redefine) and its golden path. It carries no id, clade or status of its own (YL1). The separate `ANCHOR.deed` of W3 and the course correction of W6 are withdrawn.
* *Strictness is set per verb, and `!` marks a blocking clause* [O: W7]. `Must` always blocks; `Intent` never does, except that a rule witnessed only by a refused `'Hunt` probe blocks under every verb (J18, 8.3); `Adjust`, `Trust` and `Dust` warn unless a clause carries `!`.
* *Bust findings are graded* [O: W8]: a control letter (A to E, or X) and a severity (1 to 5), before and after controls. Every grade other than A stays visible and cannot be suppressed; `X1` blocks; a repo may only tighten this.
* *Contractiles are a required A3 check* [O: W9; A3 E12].
* The deed attests where the parties are with a `(contractiles …)` clause, never the verdict [O: Q14; J6]. K9 gets an envelope ABNF [O: Q10].
* A7 adds no identity mechanism. A known shortfall is an A2 `(deviation)`, and debt is held in `debt_praxis.deed` (J9).
* A2ML leaves the estate one reader at a time and one repository at a time, never by sweep (section 11).

*Laws.*

* *YL1.* No clause defined here carries a repo id, a clade or a lifecycle state of its own. Those are A1's.
* *YL2.* No clause defined here states that the repo conforms.
* *YL3.* A descriptile never restates a contractile constraint. It references the clause by id (DESCRIPTILE-SPEC §contamination).
* *YL4.* Case is part of the rule: a file in capitals is a descriptile (what is), a Capitalised file is a contractile (what must be). A file whose case and head disagree is refused [O: W4].
* *YL5.* A descriptile edit never changes policy. To keep an observed state, redeclare the contractile [O: W5].
* *YL6.* Precedence: the contractiles outrank the descriptiles. The anchor ranks with neither: it states semantic authority and a golden path, and what satellites must not redefine is written as edicts (6.3) [O: S1].

== 6. The anchor: the `(anchor …)` clause [O: Q11, S1]

=== 6.1 Purpose

The anchor states the repo's semantic authority and its golden path: what a downstream or satellite repo may extend but not redefine, and how to bring the repo back on track. It is a clause of the chora deed, at most one, at the top level of a `repo-deed`. It is not identity: it carries no id, no clade and no status (YL1).

S1 withdrew the separate `ANCHOR.deed` file (W3) and the authorised course correction with open and released entries, drift areas and precedence over the contractiles (W6). Nothing in A7 reads an anchor as outranking a contractile.

=== 6.2 Shape

The grammar is `1-formats/deed/spec/abnf/clauses/anchor.abnf` v2.0.0 (v1.0.0 in standards#1490), checked by `deed_lint.js --anchor`. Its tests are YA01 to YA09 (12.3).

[source]
----
(repo-deed
  :schema-version "1.0.0"
  :canonical-name "example-game"
  :repo-uuid #uuid"00000000-0000-8000-8000-0000000000a1"
  ; … identity, lineage …
  (anchor
    :schema "hyperpolymath.anchor/2"
    :authority "upstream-canonical"              ; open string (J7)
    :purpose ("A game engine fully usable by developers who cannot see.")
    (metadata :version "1.0.0" :last-updated "2026-11-14")
    (semantic-authority :policy "Satellites extend the engine API, never redefine it."
                        :owns ("engine/api/"))
    (golden-path :smoke-test-command ("just test-a11y")
                 :success-criteria ("Every image in dev-ui/ has alt text."))))
----

=== 6.3 Rules beyond the grammar

* `anchor.abnf`'s SEMANTIC rules hold: `:schema` and `:authority` exactly once, every other member at most once, one clause at the top level of a repo deed, a parent (in a `/1` clause) named only by a version 8 chora id, an opaque `#uuid` or a `#u8` compared by the profile C id it names and never `#u5` (S2, YA06), never the deed itself.
* The anchor never restates a contractile. A rule that satellites must obey (`(satellite-policy …)`, `(semantic-authority …)` read as a constraint) is an edict in `Mustfile.deed` (8.1); the anchor's members are informative and no runner gates on them.
* `(golden-path :success-criteria)` items are prose. Where one must be enforced, it is written as an edict too.
* `anchor.abnf` v2.0.0 makes three changes. J29: `(ssg …)` is dropped, because site-build configuration is `META.deed`'s. J8: the nested `(identity …)` member, which names the subject of the authority and not the repo, is renamed `(subject …)`. J26: `(parents …)` is dropped, because a parent is an `ECOSYSTEM.deed` `(related … :relationship parent)` entry. A clause states its own major in `:schema`, and the profile checks it against that version (J27): `"hyperpolymath.anchor/1"` under v1.0.0, with a `legacy` warning, and `"hyperpolymath.anchor/2"` under v2.0.0. Code search on 2026-11-14 found 275 files carrying `/1`. The template moves to `/2`, and minted repos move in their own M7 PRs.

=== 6.4 Translating a legacy anchor

The legacy `ANCHOR.a2ml` described semantic authority and a golden path, which is what the clause holds. Its fields go as follows. This is the mapping spec the ultraplan's P2.5 asked for; on ratification it moves to `1-formats/deed/mappings/anchor-to-anchor-clause.adoc`.

[cols="2,3"]
|===
| Legacy field | Destination

| `id` (reverse DNS) | Dropped: the chora `:repo-uuid` is the id (YL1).
| `version` | `(anchor (metadata :version))`.
| `authority` | `(anchor :authority)`. A legacy anchor with no `authority` (standards' own has none) translates as incomplete: an error in strict mode, and the owner supplies the value in the M4 PR. The translator never invents one.
| `golden_path.*` | `(anchor (golden-path …))`, field for field.
| `semantic_authority.*`, `satellite_policy.*`, `implementation_policy.*` | `(anchor (semantic-authority …))`, `(anchor (satellite-policy …))` and `(anchor (implementation-policy …))`. Any of them that must be enforced is also written as an edict in `Mustfile.deed` (6.3).
| `clade`, `status` | Dropped from the anchor. Clade placements are identity facts in the chora (W10, A1 §11); status is `STATE.deed` (J15).
| `ssg.*` | `META.deed` `(ssg …)` (J29). `anchor.abnf` v2.0.0 has no `(ssg …)`; a `/1` anchor's is read as legacy.
| `parents` (reverse-DNS names) | `ECOSYSTEM.deed` `(related :uuid #uuid"…" :relationship parent)`, using each parent's chora id (J26). A parent with no registered id is left out, and the translation is reported incomplete: a warning in lax mode, an error in strict (J12).

== 7. The descriptiles: ALL-CAPS deeds [O: W3, W4, Q12, D312]

=== 7.1 Files, names and modules

Common members on every descriptile (DESCRIPTILE-SPEC §affirmation):

----
affirm-member = ( %s":affirmed"    token-sep date-string )   ; YYYY-MM-DD
              / ( %s":affirmed-by" token-sep affirmer )

The affirmation members are required by default, and a repo setting can relax that (J4). The one exception is an unused `CLADE.deed` (`:in-use #f`): its minimum form carries no affirmation members, and YD06 does not apply to it (S4, 7.2). The settings interface is J19 (ruled 2026-11-13, 14.2). Until the `agentic` module lands (section 11, M3), no setting can be written or checked, so the default holds everywhere. J4 supersedes the unconditional requirement in DESCRIPTILE-SPEC §affirmation, which is amended to match in a follow-up; until then, where the two differ for a relaxed repo, J4 governs.

=== 7.3 The six verb descriptiles [O: Q1, Q2, Q12, Q16]

Each verb's reality is its ALL-CAPS deed. It holds facts, each naming the edict it witnesses (J5):

[source]
----
(descriptile-deed
  :schema-version "1.0.0"
  :canonical-name "example-game"
  :of-chora #uuid"00000000-0000-8000-8000-0000000000a1"
  :affirmed "2026-11-12" :affirmed-by agent
  (fact :witnesses "adj-alt-text" :value #f :evidence measured
        :source "docs/a11y/audit-2026-11-12.adoc")
  (fact :witnesses "adj-contrast" :value "4.8:1" :evidence measured))
----

* `:witnesses` names the `:id` of a rule in the verb's `<Verb>file.deed`. That id is the join the runner uses (J5).
* `:evidence` comes from A2 D2's set. `:value` is any deed value.
* What each verb's facts are about follows the ruled meanings. `INTENT`: progress toward the north star. `MUST`: invariants. `TRUST`: security, privacy and safety. `ADJUST`: accessibility. `DUST`: exnovation: what has been stripped down, disabled, uninstalled or retired. `BUST`: failure pathways and the controls applied, graded as in 8.5.
* A fact never restates the edict it witnesses (YL3).

Debt is not a descriptile of its own. It is held in `.machine_readable/descriptiles/debt_praxis.deed`, a `praxis-deed` whose entries take the A2 deviation shape with `:kind debt` (J9).

== 8. The contractiles: verb deeds, edicts and the attestation [O: W2, W3, W7, W8, Q14]

=== 8.1 The edict

File: `.machine_readable/contractiles/<verb>/<Verb>file.deed`, head `contractile-deed` (J14), one per verb: `Intentfile.deed`, `Mustfile.deed`, `Trustfile.deed`, `Adjustfile.deed`, `Dustfile.deed`, `Bustfile.deed`. Beside it sits the verb's K9 runner, `<verb>/<verb>.k9.ncl`, with its Nickel body `<verb>/<verb>.ncl` and the shared `_base.ncl`.

`<verb>`, `<Verb>` and `<VERB>` are not one word in three cases. The verb symbol `intend` takes the stem `Intent` and the descriptile name `INTENT`, so `Intendfile.deed` and `INTEND.deed` dispatch to nothing (deed.abnf `verb-cap`, `descriptile-name`). Every path in this specification follows this table (SysArch R-14):

[cols="1,2,2"]
|===
| Verb symbol and directory `<verb>` | Edict `<Verb>file.deed` | Reality `<VERB>.deed`

| `intend` | `Intentfile.deed` | `INTENT.deed`
| `must` | `Mustfile.deed` | `MUST.deed`
| `trust` | `Trustfile.deed` | `TRUST.deed`
| `adjust` | `Adjustfile.deed` | `ADJUST.deed`
| `dust` | `Dustfile.deed` | `DUST.deed`
| `bust` | `Bustfile.deed` | `BUST.deed`
|===

Each rule is an edict with two halves [O: W2]:

[source]
----
(contractile-deed
  :schema-version "1.0.0"
  :canonical-name "example-game"
  :of-chora #uuid"00000000-0000-8000-8000-0000000000a1"
  :verb adjust
  (rule :id "adj-alt-text" !
    :text "A repo with a developer audience gives every image in its developer front end alt text."
    (applies-when (identity :audience developers))
    (requires (adjust :witness "adj-alt-text" :value #t)))
  (rule :id "adj-contrast"
    :text "Text contrast is at least 4.5:1."
    (applies-when (always))
    (requires (adjust :witness "adj-contrast" :at-least "4.5:1"))))
----

* `applies-when` tests the chora (A1 identity, and A2 profile and capabilities) and, where a rule needs it, context such as a path or a deployment. `applies-when` holds exactly one selector: `(always)`, or one of `(identity …)`, `(profile …)`, `(capabilities …)` and `(context …)` with at least one field; there are no `and`, `or` or `not` combinators (J21). `(always)` applies everywhere. It never tests a clade placement: placements are identity facts (W10), but no edict hangs off the clade, so a selector that reads `(clade …)` is refused.
* `requires` tests the verb's descriptile facts, named by witness id.
* A rule *applies* when `applies-when` holds. It is *violated* when it applies and `requires` does not hold. It is *unresolved* when it applies and no fact witnesses it.
* The same edict says nothing about a repo whose identity it does not select. Identity chooses which edicts apply; a repo does not list them.
* A rule never restates a descriptile fact, and a descriptile never restates a rule (YL3). Scope, assumptions and boundaries stay on each clause in `_base.ncl` [O: Q6].
* `!` after `:id` marks a blocking rule (8.4, J17). `!` is not a deed token in `deed.abnf` v1.4.0, and a clause profile cannot add a token, so the sample above does not parse until `deed.abnf` v1.5.0 admits it (M3, before the edict form; SysArch R-03).

The runner reads the chora, the verb's contractile deed and the verb's descriptile deed. The interim `scripts/run-mustfile.sh` already reads a Mustfile written as a `praxis-deed`; it becomes the `Mustfile.deed` reader.

=== 8.3 What the deed does not say (J6)

The deed attests where each party is. It never attests the verdict. The runner derives the verdict and writes a receipt. The contract is settled by redeclaring the contractile (W5) or by fixing the cause.

A shortfall the owner accepts for a time is an A2 deviation, never a field here:

[source]
----
(deviations
  (deviation :id "D-A7-1" :kind deferral :rule "contractile/adjust/adj-contrast"
             :reason "Theme rework lands next release." :authority "owner/standards#0000"
             :evidence measured :expires "2027-01-31" (scope)))
----

Probe execution stays in the one signed `'Hunt` component [O: Q9], under the conditions of ultraplan P0.1, which A7 carries forward unchanged:

* A signature authorises `'Hunt` only when an L3 verifier, wired through `K9_SIG_VERIFIER` with a trusted-key policy, has verified it. `'Present_Unverified` never authorises it (`K9-CONTRACT-SPEC` §10.4).
* Execution needs all five preconditions: `signature`, `policy`, `sandbox`, `dry_run` and `capability_grant`. A missing one refuses the run and is named.
* While no verifier runs in CI, probes stay refused, and the rules they would witness are blocking unresolved verdicts, whatever the rule's verb: a refused probe is missing evidence, not a reported violation. They never run on an unverified signature.

=== 8.4 Strictness per verb, and `!` [O: W7, W9]

[cols="1,2,2,2"]
|===
| Verb | Violated rule | Rule marked `!`, violated | Unresolved rule

| `must` | blocks | blocks (`!` is implied and may be omitted) | blocks
| `intend` | advisory | `!` is refused: an `Intentfile` keeps room to explore | reported
| `adjust`, `trust` | warning | blocks | warning; blocks when marked `!`
| `dust` | warning | blocks. A rule on an irreversible decision (an uninstall, a deletion, a revocation) takes `!` and blocks until the decision is recorded | warning; blocks when marked `!`
| `bust` | graded (8.5) | blocks | graded (8.5)
|===

One exception is carried from ultraplan P0.1 unchanged (8.3): a rule whose only witness is a refused `'Hunt` probe is blocking unresolved whatever its verb, `intend` included. W7 does not relax it: the owner kept P0.1 for every verb (J18, 2026-11-13).

Example of the `!` line in `Trustfile.deed`: a rule that refuses SHA-1 with nothing stronger carries `!` and blocks; a rule that notes a newer algorithm (Ed448, say) not yet adopted carries no `!` and warns.

*The A3 check* (A3 E12). The baseline check `contractiles` is required. It fails on any blocking result above and on a Bust finding graded `X1` (8.5). The open-anchor failure of v0.2.7 is withdrawn with W6 (S1); A3 §5.3 cited it and needs the same change. Every warning is reported in the verdict envelope's findings (A3 3.4) and does not fail the check. The check is `not_applicable` only for a repo that has neither a `(contractiles …)` clause nor an entry in the `contractiles-adopted` list; it is reported with that reason, never silently green and never failed (A3 §6; SysArch R-04). A rollout marker, not PR history, tells the two states apart, because A3 §7 derives the verdict only from its pinned inputs. The marker is an entry `(repo :chora #uuid"…" :from "YYYY-MM-DD")` in the `contractiles-adopted` list of A3's pinned check table (`estate-gate_praxis.deed`, A3 5.1). It lives in the table's own repo, so it cannot ride in the adopting repo's PR (YR04). The protocol is two PRs in a fixed order: first the table PR adds the entry with a `:from` date, then the repo's PR adds the clause before that date and advances its caller pin of the aggregate and the table to that table commit or later (A3 G65), so the repo never reads a table without its own entry. Before `:from` the repo is treated as unlisted (checked if the clause is present, otherwise `not_applicable`); from `:from` on, YC01 holds whatever the clause's state, using the date A3 §7 already takes as input. A late repo PR therefore fails visibly rather than leaving a window. A repo that carries the clause is checked from its first adoption, listed or not; for a repo born from the template with the clause, the entry is added at registration with `:from` the registration date. The list follows A3 5.1 (A3 v0.2.8). An entry is never removed. It always has `:from`, a valid `YYYY-MM-DD` date that may move earlier but never later. Each chora id has exactly one entry, and its `:chora` resolves to a registered repo chora in chora-registry (A3 G64). A listed repo whose caller pins the aggregate or the table at a commit before its entry was last added or changed fails, whatever its clause says; moving `:from` earlier therefore advances that floor (A3 G65). A listed repo is held to YC01, so removing the clause later fails and never restores `not_applicable`.

=== 8.5 Bust grades [O: W8]

A Bust finding records a failure pathway and the controls applied to it. Its grade is a letter and a number.

[cols="1,4"]
|===
| Part | Meaning

| Letter: control class | `A` elimination: the problem is gone, proven impossible or the feature removed; only the record remains. `B` substitution: the package or approach was changed. `C` prevention: it is stopped from happening. `D` mitigation: the risk is inherent (to the language or the industry), and a recovery that helps is in place. `E` accepted: reported as a warning only (ALARP). `X` not investigated.
| Number: severity | Classical risk scoring. `1` devastating (someone takes control of the whole system), `5` trivial (it fizzles out).
|===

A finding carries two grades: `:inherent` before controls and `:residual` after them, written for example `:inherent "E1" :residual "C1"`. The residual grade is the one compared with the repo's risk appetite.

[source]
----
(fact :witnesses "bust-oob-parser" :evidence tested
      :inherent "E1" :residual "C1"
      :justification "Bounds checks added; fuzzed 10^7 inputs."
      :source "docs/bust/oob-parser.adoc")
----

*Severity is judged against a declared use envelope.* `Bustfile.deed` states what the repo is fit for and not fit for, for example `(envelope :not-for ("safety-critical") :tolerance "0.1")`. Severity is scored inside that envelope, not against the worst possible user.

*The estate floor.* Every residual grade other than `A` stays visible: no deviation, ignore file or suppression hides it, and `:justification` is optional. A residual `X1` (known devastating, not investigated) blocks. A repo may tighten the floor in its `Bustfile.deed` (block more grades, or require `:justification`), never loosen it. A reader who thinks a grade is wrong writes in, as with an anti-virus false positive: the grade changes only by a reassessment with evidence, never by suppression.

A fuller classifier (a decision tree for choosing the letter, with a note field, for bots or people) is noted for later and not specified here.

== 9. The K9 envelope ABNF [O: Q10]

File: `1-formats/k9/spec/abnf/k9-envelope.abnf`. It grammars the envelope that `K9-CONTRACT-SPEC` §File envelope states in prose, and leaves the body to Nickel, which stays normative by reference.

----
k9-file        = magic-line header *skip-line dialect-line *line
                 ; K9-E005: the first significant line picks the dialect;
                 ; text that starts neither dialect matches no production
skip-line      = blank-line / *WSP comment-line           ; L0 skips indented comments too
blank-line     = *WSP LF
dialect-line   = ( component-start / coordination-start ) *line-char LF   ; no leading space, as in L0
magic-line     = %s"K9!" LF                                  ; K9-E001: exact, no BOM, no CR
header         = 1*4comment-line                             ; SPDX within the first five lines
comment-line   = "#" *line-char LF
spdx-line      = "#" SP %s"SPDX-License-Identifier:" SP 1*license-char LF
                 ; K9-E004 SEMANTIC: one comment-line among lines 2 to 5 is an spdx-line
line           = *line-char LF
line-char      = %x01-09 / %x0B-0C / %x0E-7F / UTF8-2 / UTF8-3 / UTF8-4
                 ; K9-E002: no NUL; K9-E003: no CR anywhere; UTF-8 well formed
license-char   = ALPHA / DIGIT / "." / "-" / "+" / "(" / ")" / SP
; K9-E006 SEMANTIC: no line longer than 998 octets, LF excluded.
; K9-E005 dialect, decided by the first significant line after the header:
component-start    = "{" / "[" / "(" / %s"let " / DQUOTE / identifier *SP "="
coordination-start = key ":" / "---"                         ; routed to coordination-k9-grammar_v1.2.abnf, which rejects "---" (D318)
identifier         = ( ALPHA / "_" ) *( ALPHA / DIGIT / "_" / "." / DQUOTE / "-" )   ; L0: [A-Za-z_][A-Za-z0-9_."-]*
key                = ( ALPHA / "_" ) *( ALPHA / DIGIT / "_" / "." / "-" )            ; L0: [A-Za-z_][A-Za-z0-9_.-]*
----

The `UTF8-n` rules are `deed.abnf`'s, unchanged. `identifier` and `key` are defined here, not imported: ABNF has no import, and the module must compile on its own for the differential. The two rules copy the character classes of `k9-validate.sh` L0 (lines 347 to 354 at the evidence commit), so that the grammar and L0 claim the same first lines. YK02 has one fixture for each dialect and one for unclaimed text, such as a first line `protocol` with no `:` or `=`, which matches no production and is K9-E005 in both. The step also amends the section of `K9-CONTRACT-SPEC` that explains why there is no `k9.abnf` (ultraplan P1.5), so that the section says the envelope has a grammar and the body does not.

The grammar must be `k9-validate.sh` L0's oracle, not a second description. Every envelope fixture under `1-formats/k9/tools/fixtures` gets the same verdict from the ABNF (through apg-js, as the deed differential does) and from L0 (test YK02).

== 11. Migration order: one repository at a time, never a sweep

Each step is one draft PR per repository, made only after the owner approves it. A step is done only when its acceptance test passes in that repository with the A2ML input absent.

. *M1, the gate.* Land Part 1a (#1488). Callers of `governance-reusable.yml` get it through the existing pin propagation; the template's inline governance gets the debt ratchet as an inline job (rsr-template-repo#372, S3), and the existing-A2ML lister once the applier moves the template's standards pin (owner ruling 2026-11-14: pins move only through the applier). === 14.2 J19, the settings interface (ruled 2026-11-13)

J4 rules that affirmation can be relaxed in settings, by named parties, with a report. It names no settings file, no form and no receipt, so two readers could each invent their own evidence. J19 gives the interface, and the owner approved it as proposed on 2026-11-13 (14.1). Until the `agentic` module lands (section 11, M3), no relaxation can be written or checked, so the J4 default (strict) holds everywhere.

[cols="1,5,2"]
|===
| Id | Proposal | Recommended

| J19
a| *Where the settings live, and what a relaxation records.*

* The settings are a `(settings …)` clause in `AGENTIC.deed`, the descriptile that answers "may I act now". The chora deed carries none, because it is identity, not configuration (D312).
* `(affirmation :may-change (<party> …) :report-to (<channel> …))`. The clause names who may change the setting and where a change is reported; it never holds the value. The value starts as required and changes only through change records, so a clause alone can never relax affirmation. A party is `owner`, `agent` or the symbol of a named role. A channel is a symbol: `feedback-o-tron`, `heron`, or a log named in the repo.
* Each change is kept as `(relaxation :seq <n> :setting <setting> :value <value> :changed-by <party> :changed-on "YYYY-MM-DD" :reported-to <channel> :receipt "<id the channel returned>")`. `:setting` is one of three: `affirmation.required`, whose `:value` is `#t` or `#f`; `affirmation.may-change`, whose `:value` is a list of parties; and `affirmation.report-to`, whose `:value` is a list of channels. The record keeps its name for all three; the last two change the policy rather than the value. `:seq` is an integer, starting at 1 and rising by one with each entry, and it orders the entries; for each setting, the accepted entry with the highest `:seq` gives its current value. An entry is never deleted, so restoring strictness (`:value #t`) is recorded the same way.
* A reader accepts an entry only when `:changed-by` is in `:may-change`, `:reported-to` is in `:report-to`, and the `:receipt` verifies with that channel as attesting this whole entry: its `:seq`, setting, value and authenticated actor. `:may-change` and `:report-to` are read as they stood at the last accepted state, never as edited in the same change: a change can never authorise itself. A change to `:may-change` or `:report-to` is such an entry, with `:setting affirmation.may-change` or `affirmation.report-to`, authorised by the parties and reported to the channels in force before it. The clause gives the starting values and is set once by the owner; after that it is not edited, and a change that edits it is refused (checked against the base version, as A5 R05 checks a registry deletion), so every policy change is a journal entry. The `:seq` values must run 1, 2, 3 … with no gap and no duplicate; a journal that breaks that is refused whole, and the value stays strict. `:changed-by` alone is self-declared, and a receipt for one entry never verifies another. An entry that fails is reported, and the value stays as the last accepted entry left it.
* Not decided here: how a channel issues its receipt. That belongs to the feedback-o-tron and heron interfaces. Until that interface is specified (encoding, authentication and a pinned verifier fixture), no receipt can verify, so no relaxation is accepted and the J4 default (strict) holds even after the `agentic` module lands.
| Approved: the `AGENTIC.deed` clause as shown. A separate `SETTINGS.deed`, a fourteenth descriptile name, was not chosen.
