= Canon and spine — the seam between `standards` and `rsr-template-repo`
:toc: left
:toclevels: 3
:revdate: 2026-10-24
:revnumber: 0.1
:status: proposal

This directory is the entry point for one subject: *how `metadatastician/standards`
and `hyperpolymath/rsr-template-repo` work as a single system.* It is written to
be found from any estate repository, by a human arriving cold, by an agent
reading conventions, or by a bot deciding whether it may add a file.

Read link:PROPOSAL.adoc[PROPOSAL] for the diagnosis and the change list. This
page is the orientation you need before that document makes sense.

== The two roles

[cols="1,1,3,2",options="header"]
|===
| Role | Repository | Owns | Must never own

| *Canon*
| `metadatastician/standards`
| The law: criteria, policy documents, and the 22 reusable workflows that
  implement them. Every gate in the estate that says "this is not allowed" is
  written here.
| A copy of any repository's own tree. The canon states rules; it does not
  hold instances.

| *Spine*
| `hyperpolymath/rsr-template-repo`
| The reference implementation and the mint: `just repo-init` produces a child
  repository, and the template's own 32 workflows are the shape a child starts
  from.
| The law. When the spine and the canon disagree about what is required, the
  canon wins and the spine is wrong — otherwise every minted child inherits the
  disagreement.
|===

Both repositories have been edited by many agents over a long time, each edit
locally reasonable. The result is two trees that each contain a partial copy of
the other's job. link:PROPOSAL.adoc#_diagnosis[The diagnosis] names six
mechanisms by which that happened; the change list addresses the mechanisms
rather than the symptoms.

== The seam, measured

The link between the two is five artefacts. It is checked in both directions —
and the two directions are pinned to *different things*, which is the defect:

[cols="2,3,1",options="header"]
|===
| Artefact | What it does today | Checked?

| Reusable pins
| Eight reusable workflows are called at one literal SHA
  (`b3ce78e1…`, 2026-10-19). A bump is a hand edit in the spine, repeated in
  every child already minted.
| at load time, but only that the SHA exists

| Second pin
| `kyaml-format.sh` is fetched from the canon at a *different* SHA
  (`75bd5999…`, 2026-10-19). One repository, two answers to "which canon".
| no

| Gate A (`canon-spine-lockstep.yml`)
| Makes the *canon* bound by the *spine*: the canon may not tighten a criterion
  the reference implementation fails.
| yes

| Job 6 of `dogfood-gate.yml`
| Fetches `canon.lock` and compares the criteria hash with the one declared in
  the spine's repo deed `(canon …)` clause.
| yes — but from **floating `main`**

| `implements`
| The declaration that job 6 reads. It lives in the deed, which is the right
  home; it names a *hash*, not a commit.
| yes, against `main`
|===

So both halves exist, and neither names a release. The canon may publish a new
lock at any moment and turn the spine red with no change to the spine; the
spine's workflows meanwhile keep running an eight-day-old copy of the canon's
CI. Green means "implements today's canon" in one file and "runs some canon" in
another, and nothing in either repository can state the difference.

Adoption of the canon by the spine is therefore a matter of habit:

[cols="1,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Measure | Value (2026-10-24) | Why it matters

| Reusable workflows the canon offers
| 22
| The menu.

| Reusable workflows the spine calls
| 8
| 36% — and the eight are the oldest ones.

| Spine workflows that call nothing
| 23 of 32
| Each is either a local implementation of a canon concern, or a genuine
  local concern. Nothing distinguishes the two, so nobody can tell which
  duplicates are deliberate.
|===

Both directions are checked, and neither check names a release: Gate A compares
the canon with the spine as it is, and job 6 compares the spine with the canon as
it is *today*. The proposal keeps both checks and gives them a commit to compare
against, so "behind" becomes a fact instead of an inference.
