[IMPORTANT]
====
*Nothing described here is implemented, installed or enforcing anything.*

This document is the *normative vocabulary and decision rules* a PR-readiness
checker must satisfy. It records the semantics half of standards#1379. The
implementing half is a separate proposal in the CI/CD suite
(link:https://github.com/hyperpolymath/proven-cicd-and-workflows/issues/63[proven-cicd-and-workflows#63]),
and it is not built either. No workflow in this repository reads this file, and
no required context depends on it.

A checker may be described as *existing and enforcing* only when it satisfies
<<maturity>>, which requires fixture-backed negative controls *and* at least
two independent consumer evaluations. Until then every claim about readiness
produced under this contract is *advisory under protest*: it is a reading taken
by a named tool at a named SHA, not a gate.

This distinction is not pedantry. {signal} §1 exists because a check that
cannot fail gets counted as assurance, and an unbuilt gate described in the
present tense is the same defect in prose.
====

== 1. Scope

=== 1.1 What this contract covers

One question, asked about one pull request at one commit:

[quote]
Is there *evidence at the current PR head* that every requirement this
repository has declared applicable was *actually executed and satisfied* — and
what exactly is missing where that is not true?

That is *PR readiness*. It is not:

* a release gate — link:../release-pre-flight/V1-GATE.adoc[`V1-GATE.adoc`] owns
  what may be called `v1.0.0` stable, and readiness here is a strictly weaker
  claim;
* a grade or score — link:../../docs/BADGE-CRITERIA-SPEC.adoc[BADGE-CRITERIA-SPEC]
  forbids a composite number, and this contract forbids one too (<<aggregation>>);
* a merge action — nothing in this contract authorises merging, and no consumer
  may treat a `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` reading as authorisation;
* a substitute for the reviewer — <<review-threads>> decides whether a thread is
  *substantively disposed*, never whether a human should approve the change.

=== 1.2 Who must read it

Whoever writes or consumes a readiness checker, and whoever configures one for a
repository. Two audiences, two obligations: <<consumer-configuration>> is what a
repository must declare, and <<states>> is what a tool must emit.

== 2. The subject: one posture at one commit

Every reading is bound to a commit. A reading that does not name its commit is
not evidence (<<output-contract>>).

[cols="1,3,4",options="header"]
|===
| Term | Definition | Consequence if absent
| `head_sha` | The PR's current head commit as observed by the checker at `checked_at`. | The reading is void. There is no "latest" state to report, and no state may be emitted except `UNKNOWN`.
| `base_sha` | The target branch commit the PR is being evaluated against. | Freshness and mergeability requirements become `UNKNOWN`, not `PASS`.
| `observed_sha` | The commit a specific piece of evidence was produced *for*. | Evidence whose `observed_sha` is not recorded cannot be matched to `head_sha` and is `UNKNOWN`.
|===

The governing rule, from which most of <<states>> follows:

[quote]
A `PASS` requires `observed_sha == head_sha`, an execution that *concluded*,
and a positive verdict. Every other combination is one of the non-PASS states,
and the checker must name which.

A push, a rebase, a force-push or a re-sign rewrites `head_sha` and therefore
*invalidates every prior reading*. Re-read after any of them; a green board that
predates the current head is the single most common false readiness signal in
this estate's history (see <<stale-head>>).

== 3. The seven states {#states}

These seven words are the whole vocabulary. They are *not* a scale and have no
ordering — any attempt to rank them, weight them or average them is a defect
(<<aggregation>>).

[cols="1,2,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| State | Meaning | Assigned when | May it reach `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`?
| `PASS` | The requirement was *executed* at `head_sha` and *succeeded*. | A check run or job with `conclusion == success`, a `status == success` context, or a validator exit 0, whose `observed_sha == head_sha`, whose run *started jobs*. | Yes — the only state that may.
| `FAIL` | The requirement was *executed* at `head_sha` and did not succeed. | `conclusion` in `failure`, `timed_out`, `action_required`, or a validator exit non-zero. Also: a *zero-job startup failure* (<<zero-job>>). | No. Hard blocker when the requirement is hard (<<tiers>>).
| `BLOCKED` | The requirement *cannot be satisfied by anyone* until something outside the diff changes. | A required context no workflow can publish; a required signature the platform refuses; a permission, plan, or tool the runner cannot obtain; a phantom requirement. | No. Never repaired by an edit to the PR.
| `PENDING` | The requirement has not concluded *yet*, and nothing observed says it will fail. | `status` in `queued`, `in_progress`, `waiting`, `requested`; or a required context that has not reported and is still producible. | No — not *yet*. Expected to resolve without human action.
| `UNKNOWN` | The checker could not determine the state. | An API failure, rate limit, truncated response, unreadable payload, missing evidence, no `head_sha`, or evidence with no `observed_sha`. | No. Fail-closed: inability to inspect is not a pass.
| `NOT_APPLICABLE` | The requirement does not apply to this PR, *for a stated reason in the policy*. | An applicability predicate in the consumer policy evaluated false, recordable and reviewable. | Yes — it is excluded from the required set, not waived.
| `ADVISORY` | The requirement was evaluated but is *non-blocking by declaration*. | Its tier in the consumer policy is advisory, or the producer is classified advisory (<<tiers>>). | Yes, but it must *never* be rendered as a pass (<<advisory-is-not-pass>>).
|===

=== 3.1 `PASS` is a claim about execution, not about appearance

The recurring defect this contract exists to stop: a green tick that summarises
*absence* of work.

==== A skipped or neutral job is not `PASS`

GitHub reports `conclusion: skipped` for a job whose `if:` evaluated false, and
`neutral` for a step that exited 78. Both render as a non-red icon, and a
`skipped` check run *satisfies* a required status check on GitHub.

That platform behaviour is exactly why this contract must not inherit it. A
skipped job means *the work did not happen*. Therefore:

* a job skipped by a `relevance` gate that decided "not relevant" is
  `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and the gate itself must be `PASS` — the gate is the thing
  that vouches for the absence, so it cannot also be the thing that is absent;
* a job skipped for any other reason — a failed `needs`, a missing matrix
  dimension, an `if:` nobody reasoned about — is `PENDING` if it can still run
  and `UNKNOWN` if it cannot, *never* `PASS`;
* `neutral` is treated as `FAIL` for a hard requirement and `ADVISORY` for an
  advisory one. A step that exits 78 has decided not to decide.

==== A required check that never reported is not green

A required context with no check run at `head_sha` is `PENDING` while it remains
publishable and `BLOCKED` when nothing on `pull_request` (or
`pull_request_target`) can publish it. It is never `NOT_APPLICABLE` and never
`PASS`. The classification of *why* it is unproducible is already computed by
{required-contexts}, and <<state-mapping>> fixes how its verdicts map onto
these states rather than inventing a second vocabulary.

== 4. Decision rules for the cases that go wrong

=== 4.1 Stale-head evidence {#stale-head}

*Rule.* Evidence whose `observed_sha != head_sha` is `PENDING` when it is being
regenerated and `UNKNOWN` when it is not, and it is *never* `PASS`.

A `PASS` observed at an ancestor of `head_sha` is *history*, not evidence about
the current diff. It is reported as a distinct annotation — `STALE_HEAD` — with
both SHAs, so a reader can see the reading is about `a1b2c3d` while the PR is
now at `d4e5f6a`.

Do not let a "last successful run" from an older head fill a current-head slot.
This is precisely the shape that makes a force-push after a passing run look
ready.

=== 4.2 Zero-job startup failures {#zero-job}

*Rule.* A workflow run with `total_count == 0` jobs, or a *required* workflow
whose run exists but no job exists, is `FAIL` with subclass `STARTUP_FAILURE` —
*not* `UNKNOWN` and not "still pending".

Justification, measured in this estate and recorded in
link:../../docs/GOVERNANCE-STARTUP-REPAIR.adoc[GOVERNANCE-STARTUP-REPAIR]:
GitHub reports a startup failure as `conclusion: failure` (not
`startup_failure`), with *zero jobs*, with run `name` equal to the run `path`,
and with no reason in REST or GraphQL — only on the run page. It therefore
*looks* like a red check in some views and like nothing at all in others, and a
view that lists only check runs shows no check for it at all.

A consumer must therefore read *workflow runs* as well as check runs, and must
treat "the required workflow has no job" as a failure to start, because that is
what it is. This is a `FAIL`, not a `BLOCKED`: the cause is in the repository's
own files (a lock the runner rejects, a callee ref that does not resolve), and
the repository can fix it.

=== 4.3 API and read failures {#api-failures}

*Rule.* Any inability to read evidence is `UNKNOWN` for the affected
requirement, and the checker records the failure class: `RATE_LIMITED`,
`HTTP_ERROR`, `TRUNCATED`, `MALFORMED`, `NOT_AUTHORISED`, or `NO_DATA`.

`UNKNOWN` is *not* `PENDING`: nothing says it will resolve, and no amount of
waiting is the repair. `UNKNOWN` is *not* `BLOCKED` either — `BLOCKED` names a
requirement nobody can satisfy, `UNKNOWN` names a checker that could not look.

A checker must never silently omit a requirement it failed to read. An omitted
row and a green row are indistinguishable to a human reader, which makes silent
omission the most dangerous output the tool can produce.

=== 4.4 Required-but-advisory is a configuration error, not a state {#tier-conflict}

*Rule.* If the repository's ruleset *requires* a context that the consumer policy
declares advisory, that is a `BLOCKED` requirement with subclass
`TIER_CONFLICT`, and the repair is to reconcile the two declarations. It is not
`ADVISORY` just because the policy file says so: the platform will still block
the merge on it, and reporting it as non-blocking would be a false readiness.

== 5. Signatures

Signatures are their own evidence class because the platform's three outcomes
are routinely conflated ({signing}, "Who signs what").

[cols="1,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Platform verdict | State | Rule
| `Verified` | `PASS` for a signature requirement | Only when the verification is against a key valid for the committer. A signature from an unregistered vendor key is *not* this.
| `Unverified`, or no signature on a required commit | `FAIL` | A missing signature on a commit that must be signed is a real, fixable failure.
| `Unknown key` (verification impossible) | `BLOCKED` with subclass `UNVERIFIABLE_KEY` | The platform cannot classify a signature whose key it does not hold. This is *not* `FAIL` (nothing says the signature is bad) and emphatically *not* `PASS`.
| No signature requirement configured | `NOT_APPLICABLE` | With the policy predicate named, and listed in the shrink-only register so a later removal is visible.
|===

Do not equate a *signed-looking* string in a commit message with a verified
signature; the signature is a property of the commit object and the key, not of
its text. {signing} owns which *writer* must use which key; this contract only
fixes how a verdict becomes a state.

== 6. Review threads {#review-threads}

=== 6.1 Substantive versus non-substantive

A thread is *substantive* when it asks the author to change the change, supply
evidence, or answer a question. It is *non-substantive* when it is a greeting, a
bot's summary banner, a link-back, a "thanks", or an automated "I will review
this" placeholder.

The checker cannot judge this reliably, and must not pretend to. Therefore:

* the *classification* is a declared function of the consumer policy
  (<<consumer-configuration>>): an explicit list of bot logins and marker
  strings treated as non-substantive;
* anything not matched by that list is *substantive by default* — the
  conservative direction, because a wrongly-substantive thread costs a reply and
  a wrongly-non-substantive thread costs a defect;
* a non-substantive thread that is *unresolved* is `ADVISORY`; a substantive
  thread that is unresolved is `FAIL` for a hard review requirement.

=== 6.2 What "resolved" means

[cols="2,1,3",options="header"]
|===
| Condition | State | Note
| Thread resolved, disposition recorded | `PASS` | A resolution is only evidence if something says how it was disposed: a fix, or an evidence-backed non-change.
| Thread resolved with no disposition recorded | `ADVISORY` | Resolving without saying why is the silent-close defect. Visible, not blocking.
| Thread `isOutdated` only, never resolved | `FAIL` | Outdated means the diff moved past it. Outdated is *not* answered, and the question may still stand.
| Substantive thread open | `FAIL` | Includes bot findings (`CodeRabbit`, `CodeQL`, `Sonar`) — a bot thread is evidence from a real tool, not noise to be ignored because a machine wrote it.
|===

`CHANGES_REQUESTED` is `FAIL` for a hard requirement regardless of the thread
state: a reviewer who requested changes has not been answered by resolutions
elsewhere. Withdrawal of the review, not a reply, is its repair.

=== 6.3 Approvals

An approval is evidence that a human reviewed, *only* where the consumer policy
requires one. It is never evidence that a scientific, proof or test requirement
passed, and it never substitutes for one. Where no approval requirement is
configured the requirement is `NOT_APPLICABLE`, not `PASS`.

== 7. Requirement tiers: hard blockers versus advisory {#tiers}

Every requirement carries a tier, and the tier decides whether the state can
block `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`.

[cols="1,2,4",options="header"]
|===
| Tier | Blocks readiness? | Which requirements
| `hard` | Yes — any of `FAIL`, `BLOCKED`, `PENDING`, `UNKNOWN` prevents readiness | Required status contexts; zero-job startup failures of required workflows; required signatures; required approvals; substantive unresolved threads where review is required; any requirement the *platform* enforces
| `advisory` | No — reported, never averaged in | Non-required scans, informational linters, trend and posture checks, bot summaries, non-substantive threads, scorecard-style repository measurements
|===

The tier is declared by the consumer, *except* for one constraint that the
consumer may not override: a requirement the platform actually enforces is
`hard` no matter what the policy file says (<<tier-conflict>>). A policy cannot
demote a merge blocker by asserting it, and the checker must detect the
conflict rather than respect the assertion.

[NOTE]
====
An advisory requirement that *fails* must still be surfaced, in its own section,
with its evidence. "Not a blocker" and "not shown" are different things, and
conflating them is how advisory findings disappear. See <<advisory-is-not-pass>>.
====

== 8. Aggregation: worst-of, never a percentage {#aggregation}

[quote]
There is no readiness score. A consumer that prints a percentage has implemented
a different product.

The overall state is computed by a fixed precedence over the applicable
requirements, worst first:

[cols="1,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Rank | Resulting overall state | Condition over applicable `hard` requirements
| 1 | *not ready — `BLOCKED`* | any `BLOCKED`
| 2 | *not ready — `FAIL`* | else any `FAIL`
| 3 | *not ready — `UNKNOWN`* | else any `UNKNOWN`
| 4 | *not ready — `PENDING`* | else any `PENDING`
| 5 | *`READY_FOR_APPROVAL`* | every applicable hard requirement is `PASS` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`
|===

`BLOCKED` outranks `FAIL` deliberately: a reading that contains a requirement
nobody can satisfy is not usefully described as "has a failure", because the
failure may be incidental while the blocker is not.

`ADVISORY` requirements never move the overall state. They are printed beside
it.

Any stated rationale of the form "only 2 of 30 checks are red", "90% green", or
"all required checks pass" without listing them is a violation of this section,
not a summary of it.

== 9. Advisory is not a pass {#advisory-is-not-pass}

An `ADVISORY` row must not be rendered with the same glyph, word or colour as a
`PASS`. The rule exists because the failure is silent: an advisory green looks
identical to a hard green, so a reader counts coverage that does not exist.

* advisory rows are printed in a separate block with the word `ADVISORY` in the
  status column;
* an advisory check that *passed* is reported as `ADVISORY (passed)`, never as
  `PASS`;
* a skipped advisory check is `ADVISORY (did not run)`;
* no count of passing advisories may be presented as evidence of assurance.

== 10. Modularity: the contract is shared, the policy is local {#modularity}

This is the section that keeps the contract adoptable, and it is normative.

=== 10.1 Nothing estate-specific is required by default

A consumer policy starts *empty of estate-specific requirements*. In
particular, a default policy must not require, and a checker must not assume:

* K9 semantic conformance, DEED rules, or any verdict-grid lane;
* Hypatia symbolic or neural findings, or gitbot-fleet report-backs;
* any proof language or prover from link:../PROOF-POLICY.adoc[PROOF-POLICY] —
  Creusot, SPARK, Idris2, Agda, Rocq or Lean;
* `proven-tests-and-benches` grades;
* any particular CI suite, reusable workflow, or naming convention.

Each of these is *available* to a consumer that wants it, and *off* until the
consumer names it. A checker that reds an upstream repository for not carrying
this estate's governance artifacts has misconfigured itself, not evaluated the
repository.

=== 10.2 Upstream owner's policy outranks the consumer's defaults

Where a contribution targets a repository the contributor does not own, the
*upstream's* declared requirements are authoritative. This contract governs how
evidence is *read*, not what any particular repository must require. If the
upstream requires nothing, the reading is `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` with a thin
evidence set — and that is a correct result, not a gap to be filled with estate
policy.

Contributed assurance that the upstream does not require is `ADVISORY` there,
and may be run in the contributor's fork. It is never a condition on the
upstream's merge.

=== 10.3 `NOT_APPLICABLE` must be justified and shrink-only

Every `NOT_APPLICABLE` names the policy predicate that excluded it. A register
of exclusions is *shrink-only*: adding one is a change to what the repository
promises, so it appears in the diff and is reviewed like one. A checker must
report a `NOT_APPLICABLE` whose predicate no longer excludes anything as stale,
the same way {required-contexts} reports a declared-but-absent context.

== 11. What a consumer must configure {#consumer-configuration}

A consumer supplies exactly one *policy document* and nothing else. The
contract fixes its meaning; the consumer fixes its contents.

[cols="1,1,4",options="header"]
|===
| Field | Required? | Meaning
| `schema` | yes | Names this contract and its version. An unknown version is `UNKNOWN`, never "assume latest".
| `repository` | yes | Canonical `owner/repo`, so a reading cannot be replayed against the wrong repository.
| `requirements[]` | yes | One entry per requirement: `id`, `tier` (`hard` or `advisory`), `kind`, and the evidence class that satisfies it.
| `requirements[].kind` | yes | One of `status-context`, `workflow`, `signature`, `review-thread`, `approval`, `validator-output`.
| `requirements[].applies_when` | no | An explicit predicate. Absent means *always applies* — the fail-closed default.
| `non_substantive[]` | no | Bot logins and marker strings excluded from <<review-threads>>. Absent means *everything substantive*.
| `signature_scope` | no | Which commits must be signed. Absent means no signature requirement (`NOT_APPLICABLE`, registered).
| `approval_policy` | no | Count and identity of required approvals. Absent means none required.
|===

=== 11.1 What a consumer may *not* configure

* a requirement the platform enforces, as advisory (<<tier-conflict>>);
* a `PASS` without an `observed_sha`;
* suppression of an `UNKNOWN` or `BLOCKED` row — it may be *declared* in the
  exclusion register with a reason, but it may not be deleted from the output;
* an `ADVISORY` tier on a required status context;
* a percentage or score in place of <<aggregation>>.

=== 11.2 Minimum viable configuration

A consumer that configures nothing but its required status contexts still gets a
valid reading: those contexts as `hard`, everything else absent, `UNKNOWN` where
the checker could not look. That is the intended adoption path — the contract is
useful at its smallest, and grows by declaration.

The example below is *illustrative of shape only*. It deliberately contains no
estate-specific requirement, and a real consumer for an upstream scientific
repository would contain fewer still.

[source,json]
----
{
  "schema": "prr-1",
  "repository": "example-owner/example-repo",
  "requirements": [
    { "id": "required-contexts", "tier": "hard", "kind": "status-context" },
    { "id": "build-and-test",     "tier": "hard", "kind": "workflow",
      "workflow": "ci.yml" },
    { "id": "signed-commits",     "tier": "hard", "kind": "signature" },
    { "id": "review-threads",     "tier": "hard", "kind": "review-thread" }
  ],
  "non_substantive": ["dependabot[bot]", "github-actions[bot]"],
  "signature_scope": "head-commit",
  "approval_policy": { "required": 1 }
}
----

== 12. State mapping for existing verdicts {#state-mapping}

The estate already computes verdicts about required contexts in
{required-contexts}. This contract does *not* invent a second vocabulary for
the same question; it fixes the mapping so a consumer reads both the same way.

[cols="2,1,4",options="header"]
|===
| Existing verdict | State | Note
| `producible` | — (no state) | This is a predicate about a *name*, not evidence about a commit. It removes the `BLOCKED` reason; it does not produce a `PASS`.
| `app-owned` | `UNKNOWN` until the app reports | A context published by a third-party app is that app's to publish. If it never reports, the requirement is `UNKNOWN` (could not observe), not `BLOCKED`.
| `PR-UNPRODUCIBLE` | `BLOCKED` | The name is satisfiable but nothing publishes it on the event that gates the merge. Repair is a trigger or a different required name — never a rename.
| `UNSATISFIABLE` | `BLOCKED` | Nothing publishes the name at all. A phantom requirement.
| `UNSATISFIED-RULE` | `BLOCKED` | A data-dependent rule whose data nothing uploads. Distinct verdict, distinct repair.
|===

== 13. The output contract {#output-contract}

A checker emits two artefacts from one evaluation. Neither is valid alone.

=== 13.1 Machine record

One record per evaluation, JSON, I-JSON + JCS per
link:../JSON-POLICY.adoc[`JSON-POLICY.adoc`]:

[cols="1,1,4",options="header"]
|===
| Field | Required | Meaning
| `contract`, `contract_version` | yes | `prr-1`.
| `repository`, `pr`, `base_sha`, `head_sha` | yes | The subject. Absent `head_sha` means the whole record is `UNKNOWN` and says why.
| `checked_at` | yes | RFC 3339 UTC. A reading is a timestamped observation, not a standing fact.
| `overall` | yes | One of `IN_PROGRESS`, `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`, `MERGED` — plus the blocking state that produced it. Never a percentage.
| `requirements[]` | yes | One entry per *configured* requirement, including ones the checker failed to read. No silent omission.
| `requirements[].state` | yes | One of the seven, verbatim from <<states>>.
| `requirements[].observed_sha` | when state is `PASS` | Mandatory. A `PASS` without it is rejected as malformed.
| `requirements[].evidence` | when state is `PASS` | URL, run id, check name, or validator output reference.
| `requirements[].subclass` | when applicable | `STARTUP_FAILURE`, `TIER_CONFLICT`, `UNVERIFIABLE_KEY`, `STALE_HEAD`, or a failure class from <<api-failures>>.
| `requirements[].why` | when state is not `PASS` | The reason, in words. A non-PASS row with no reason is malformed.
| `exclusions[]` | yes (may be empty) | Every `NOT_APPLICABLE` with its predicate, and the register of declared exclusions.
|===

=== 13.2 Human draft

A concise Markdown draft for review, shaped by
link:REPORT-TEMPLATE.adoc[`REPORT-TEMPLATE.adoc`]: overall state first, then
hard requirements, then a *separate* advisory block, then exclusions, then the
SHAs and the timestamp. Every non-PASS row links its evidence or states its
reason. Any `BLOCKED` or `UNKNOWN` appears plainly — never folded into a
narrative of progress.

=== 13.3 The three human-facing states

[cols="1,3,3",options="header"]
|===
| State | Meaning | Transition rule
| `IN_PROGRESS` | The engineering work is not finished, or any applicable hard requirement is not `PASS`/`NOT_APPLICABLE`. | Default. The starting state of every PR.
| `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` | Every applicable hard requirement is `PASS` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`, at the current `head_sha`, and no substantive thread is unresolved. | Only from a reading at the *current* head. Any new commit returns the PR to `IN_PROGRESS`.
| `MERGED` | The change is integrated into the target branch and post-merge validation was *observed*. | Requires an actual target-branch SHA and an observed post-merge result. A merge button press is not this state.
|===

`MERGED` deserves emphasis: it is assertable only from *observation of the
target branch*, never from "the merge succeeded" as narrated by a tool. The
distinction is the same one <<stale-head>> draws for the head.

== 14. Checker self-conduct

A checker is subject to the same standard it applies. It must:

* *fail closed* — exit non-zero on inability to inspect, and never print `PASS`
  over zero inspected requirements. An empty population is a `NOT_INSPECTED`
  condition, not a clean one;
* *state its population* — how many requirements it read, of how many
  configured;
* *be read-only* — no resolve, no approve, no merge, no dismissal, no write of
  any kind, against any PR;
* *name itself and its version* in every record, so a later reader can tell
  which checker produced a reading;
* *refuse to claim enforcement* until <<maturity>> is satisfied.

== 15. Maturity: when a checker may be called enforcing {#maturity}

The suite proposal (proven-cicd-and-workflows#63) owns the implementation. This
contract owns what "done" means, so that "done" is not asserted by whoever is
impatient.

[cols="1,2,3",options="header"]
|===
| Stage | Entry condition | What it may claim
| *0. Vocabulary* (this document) | Contract ratified. | "The semantics are specified."
| *1. Implemented* | A checker reads fixtures and emits the machine record + human draft. | "The checker runs on saved data."
| *2. Controlled* | Fixture-backed negative controls for every case in <<negative-controls>>, each with a planted-positive counterpart proving the control can fail. | "The checker refuses readiness on known-bad input."
| *3. Evaluated* | At least *two independent consumer evaluations* on distinct repositories, with before/after classifications reported, including at least one failing sample. | "The checker has been tested against reality twice."
| *4. Enforcing* | Stage 3, plus a declared tier in the consumer's policy, plus the consumer's own decision to require it. | "This repository requires it."
|===

Until stage 4 for a given repository, everything is `ADVISORY` by
configuration — and per <<advisory-is-not-pass>>, reported as `ADVISORY`, never
as a pass. The suite's own GOVERNANCE requires two real repositories before
merging a gate; <<maturity>> is the readiness-specific restatement of that rule,
not a replacement for it.

=== 15.1 Required negative controls {#negative-controls}

A checker that passes these controls only in the positive direction has not been
tested. Each must have a fixture that must *refuse* readiness, and a planted
positive that must be *ready*, so that the control can fail:

. required job skipped;
. required context absent entirely;
. `PASS` observed at an older head (`STALE_HEAD`);
. a validator that failed inside a green summary wrapper;
. zero-job workflow startup failure;
. API/read failure mid-evaluation;
. an unverified required signature, and an `unknown key` signature;
. an unresolved substantive review thread;
. `CHANGES_REQUESTED` with all threads resolved;
. a required context the policy calls advisory (tier conflict);
. a `PASS` row emitted without `observed_sha` (malformed-record rejection);
. an empty population (zero requirements inspected).

The suite proposal's list and this one agree; where they differ, the union is
required, and a missing control is a reason to withhold stage 2.

== 16. Coordination with the implementation

[cols="1,3,4",options="header"]
|===
| Owner | Owns | Must not own
| This document (standards#1379) | The seven states, their decision rules, tier semantics, aggregation, modularity, the output contract, and the maturity gate. | The implementation, its language, its fixture layout, or its release schedule.
| Suite proposal (proven-cicd-and-workflows#63) | The read-only adapter, fixture format, live-data capture behind narrow scopes, and the CLI. | The meaning of a state, or permission to widen a state's definition to make a repository pass.
| The consumer repository | Which requirements it declares, their tiers, its exclusions, and whether it enforces. | A state's meaning, or the tier of a requirement the platform itself enforces.
|===

Readiness semantics that turn out to be wrong are repaired *here* first, then in
the checker. A checker that has diverged from this document is out of
conformance even if it is green, and the divergence is the finding.

MetaManifold PR #306 is an example consumer, not the specification. A defect
found while adopting this contract in a real PR is fixed in that PR when it is a
blocker, and otherwise tracked in the owning repository — it is not absorbed
into the framework, and it is not a reason to hold the PR.

== 17. Non-goals and limits

* *No gate yet.* Stated in the banner, repeated here so a reader arriving at
  this section does not miss it.
* *No claim about any named tool.* This document does not assert that any
  existing workflow, badge, dashboard or bot computes these states correctly.
* *Not a substitute for the platform.* The ruleset is authoritative about what
  blocks a merge. This contract describes how to *read* that authority; a
  disagreement between the two is a `TIER_CONFLICT` to be reconciled, not
  something the checker may resolve by preferring itself.
* *Not proof of correctness.* `PASS` on a test requirement means the test suite
  ran and exited zero at that SHA. It does not mean the change is correct, and
  the vocabulary deliberately has no state that means "correct".
* *Advisory classification is lossy by design.* <<review-threads>> asks the
  consumer to *declare* what is non-substantive, which is a judgement. The
  default-substantive rule bounds the damage; it does not remove the judgement.
* *This contract does not opt into doctrine coverage.* A
  `// doctrine-coverage: on` marker requires either an `Enforced-by:` path or a
  `Review-only:` reason for every normative heading. Nothing enforces this
  document yet, so every tag would be `Review-only:` — true but unhelpful forty
  times over — and an `Enforced-by:` tag pointing at a checker that does not
  exist would be exactly the decorative claim <<maturity>> forbids. The marker
  lands in the change that brings the checker to stage 2, with real paths behind
  the tags that earn one.
