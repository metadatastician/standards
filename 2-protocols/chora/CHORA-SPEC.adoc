== 1. Summary

* Identity is the chora at 3 levels: *forge*, *estate*, *repo* [O, R4].
* Forge has 2 kinds: a *federation root* and a *hosting platform* [O].
* The federation root is the single declaration site of vocabulary. Estates extend it and never re-declare it [O].
* A repo chora beholds exactly 1 estate chora, and may declare co-estates. The index is therefore a graph with 1 primary edge per repo [O].
* The index (`ATLAS.deed`) is generated from the beholding relation and is never hand-edited [O]. It is the estate's finding index and lives in chora-registry, the one registry; the office reads it (Q3 [O]).
* A repo's clade placements are identity facts in its chora: where the repo sits. They are never its id and never an edict. chora-registry (the renamed `gv-clade-index`) holds the estate-wide classification schemes and is authoritative [O: A7 W10, amending R4].
* The deed core stays small. A later trope format adds term kinds and no grammar [O].

== 3. The hierarchy

----
  federation root   federation_forge.deed   (forge-deed, :kind federation)
        |  declares vocabulary, root invariants, the estate set
        |
        +--- platform chora   <stem>_forge.deed   (forge-deed, :kind platform)
        |       github.com, gitlab.com, codeberg.org, bitbucket.org
        |
        +--- estate chora     estate_chora.deed   (estate-deed)
                |  extends vocabulary, adds estate invariants, names its hosting namespaces
                |
                +--- repo chora   <repo>_chora.deed   (repo-deed)
                        beholds 1 estate; may declare co-estates
----

A platform chora records a hosting platform and its role. The 4 platforms in the estate record are github.com (primary) and gitlab.com, codeberg.org, bitbucket.org (mirrors) [R: forges.a2ml]. == 4. Answers to the 5 open questions

[cols="1,3,3"]
|===
| Question | Answer [O] | Rule

| 1. Which clauses does the estate chora require?
| `(vocabulary)` and `(invariants)`, with types, tropes and lattice as `:kind` values of a term.
| The declaration of vocabulary sits at the federation root (answer to the declaration-site question below). The estate chora requires `(extends-vocabulary)`, `(invariants)` and `(hosting)`. See 5.2 and C1.

| 2. How is vocabulary versioned?
| Semver plus content hash.
| The root declares `:vocabulary-version` (semver string). An estate pins `:min` in `(extends-vocabulary)`. The index records the sha256 of the root's bytes. See 8.3.

| 3. May a repo chora behold several estate choras?
| One, plus declared co-ownership.
| `:beholding-chora` has exactly 1 value. Optional `(co-estates ...)` lists further estates. The index is a graph. See 6.

| 4. K9 and estate invariants conflict: which wins?
| The effective set is the meet of the two sets.
| The validator computes the meet. K9 may add prohibitions and may never remove one. See 10.

| 5. How does ATLAS relate to the chora hierarchy?
| ATLAS is the generated index.
| `ATLAS.deed` is the output of the index generator. See 8.
|===

Declaration site over several estates [O]: vocabulary is declared once at the federation root. An estate may add terms only through `(extends-vocabulary)`, and a term it adds MUST NOT collide with a root term name. This settles the "extends-vocabulary" item that the deed spec lists as open. It replaces the rule "vocabulary is declared in `estate_chora.deed` and nowhere else" with "vocabulary is declared at the root, and extended only by an estate through a clause the root can verify".

Every deed carries `:schema-version` exactly once [grammar]. The ABNF is authoritative [D308]. A1 proposes 1 additive grammar change (section 7), and a change to the identity table of the deed spec (section 7.2). Both need approval.

=== 5.1 Federation root: `federation_forge.deed`

|===
| Element | Kind | Rule

| `:canonical-name` | field | STRING. Example: `"hyperpolymath-federation"`.

| `:vocabulary-version` | field | STRING, semver.

| `(invariants (rule ...))` | clause | 0 or more `rule`. `rule` fields: `:id` STRING (unique in the federation; added by A2 D1, approved), `:priority` INTEGER (unique in the clause), `:text` STRING.
| `(publishers (publisher ...))` | clause | Optional. Trusted verdict publishers (A3 E1). A rotation adds the new entry before it removes the old one.
| `(deviations (deviation ...))` | clause | Optional, once (A2 section 7).
| `(estates (estate ...))` | clause | The estate set in scope. `estate` fields: `:name` STRING, `:uuid` an opaque `#uuid` literal.
|===

The root has no `:beholding-chora`. It is the top of the hierarchy. There is exactly 1 root in a federation.

=== 5.2 Estate chora: `estate_chora.deed`

[cols="2,1,4"]

== 6. Beholding and co-ownership

. `:beholding-chora` resolves to the primary estate. A validator MUST resolve it by UUID, never by file name.
. `(co-estates)` adds graph edges. A co-estate edge does not give the repo vocabulary or invariants. Only the primary estate does that.
. Each co-estate edge MUST be confirmed from the other side. Confirmation [O, C7]: the co-estate's estate chora lists the repo's UUID in an optional `(co-repos (co-repo :uuid <repo-uuid>))` clause. Until it does, an unconfirmed edge is reported with state `unconfirmed` and is not an error.
. A praxis deed (A2 pack manifests, A3 check table, A4 work orders, A5 rule registries) names an estate chora in `:beholding-chora`, as the deed spec requires. Its value is an `:estate-uuid`.
. A transfer changes `:beholding-chora` and records the previous estate under `(lineage)`. The repo UUID does not change (section 9).

== 7. Grammar proposals (not edits to the ABNF)

=== 7.1 `deed.abnf` v1.3.0, additive (current grammar v1.4.0, also additive)

* New head: `forge-deed`.
* New filename production: `forge-file = stem %s"_forge.deed"`, dispatching to `forge-deed`. It is tested after `estate-file` and does not overlap `repo-file` or `praxis-file`.
* New head list entry and one new line in the semantic table of the head comment.
* New value: the opaque UUID literal (ruling U4 [O]).
+
----
value         =/ uuid
uuid          = %s"#uuid" DQUOTE uuid-hex DQUOTE
uuid-hex      = 8hex-lc "-" 4hex-lc "-" uuid-version 3hex-lc "-"
                uuid-variant 3hex-lc "-" 12hex-lc
uuid-version  = "5" / "8"
uuid-variant  = "8" / "9" / %s"a" / %s"b"
hex-lc        = DIGIT / %x61-66   ; lowercase a to f only
----
+
As adopted in v1.3.0 the grammar names these `uuid`, `uuid-hex` and `hex-lc`, and checks the version and variant digits itself (the draft here had them as a validator rule). The rule: the version digit (the first digit of the third group) is `5` or `8`, and the variant digit (the first digit of the fourth group) is `8`, `9`, `a` or `b` (the RFC 4122 variant). Versions 5 and 8 only (C10 [O]): they are the name-based versions that the registry mints today (`uuid` and `uuid_v8`), and `deed.abnf` v1.3.0 admits both [M]. Version 4 (random) and version 7 (time-ordered) are excluded, because an identity is minted from a birth path and must be reproducible from the registry archive. In an identity field the value is version 8 (section 9, rule V8 [O]); a version 5 value there is reported `v5-identity`, an error in every mode, `lax` included (owner ruling of 2026-11-13, restated by Q2).
* *Q2 [O].* Owner ruling of 2026-11-14 (SysArch Q2, scope and timing confirmed by selector in the A1 thread the same day). `#u5"..."` and `#u8"..."` stay legal values outside identity fields. In an identity field, `#u8"domain:name"` is accepted, because it computes to a v8 id, and `#u5"..."` is reported `u5-identity`, an error in every mode, `lax` included. This covers references as well as a deed's own id, so the legacy beholding value `#u5"estate/chora"` is an error too, and its `lax` acceptance (T32) is withdrawn. `scripts/check-uuid-v8.sh` enforces this from 2026-11-14. It replaces the `deprecated-identity-literal` warning. The identity fields are:
** a deed's own id: `:repo-uuid` (`repo-deed`), `:estate-uuid` (`estate-deed`) and `:forge-uuid` (`forge-deed`);
** the reference to the estate or root chora it belongs to: `:beholding-chora` (`repo-deed`, `estate-deed`, a platform `forge-deed`, `praxis-deed`), and `:of-chora` (the heads of A7 J14);
** clause fields that name a chora: `(extends-vocabulary :base)`, `(hosting (namespace :forge))`, `(estates (estate :uuid))`, `(co-estates (co-estate :estate))` and `(co-repos (co-repo :uuid))`; in `ATLAS.deed`, `(estate :uuid)`, `(repo :uuid)` and `(co-estate :estate)`; in an A4 work order, `(work-order :repo)`; and in `ECOSYSTEM.deed`, `(related :uuid)`, which is where a parent repo is named (A7 J26).
** The rule goes by field name, not by clause. The owner ruled on 2026-11-14, by selector, that the checker and `deed_lint` both flag one of these names followed by `#u5` (or by a version 5 `#uuid`) wherever it appears, in prose and comments too. The names are therefore reserved for chora ids in every deed: an id that is not a chora id takes another name, such as `:alias-id`.
* Semantic rule (validator, not ABNF): a `forge-deed` has exactly 1 `:kind` field, whose value is `federation` or `platform`. The stem `federation` is legal only for `:kind federation`, and exactly 1 such file exists per federation.

Every v1.2.1 deed stays valid. The `deed_lint.js` head whitelist, filename dispatch and value parser need the same additions. `deed.abnf` v1.3.0 (#1473, merged 2026-11-12) adopted the opaque literal with versions 5 and 8, and its linter accepts the samples as written [M]. The forge head is not in v1.3.0 and the linter does not dispatch it, so the forge samples are checked only in the adapted form noted beside them [M].

== 8. The generated index

=== 8.1 What it is

`ATLAS.deed` (head `estate-atlas-deed`) is the output of an index generator [O]. It is a deterministic function of: the federation root, the estate chora of each estate in the root's `(estates)` clause, every platform chora, and every repo chora found under each estate's `(hosting)` namespaces. Nobody edits it. It lives in chora-registry, today `hyperpolymath/gv-clade-index` until the rename lands, and the office reads it from there (Q3 [O], 2026-11-14, replacing the plan's default of `git-logistics-office`).

How the generator finds repo choras is not part of A1. It can enumerate repos in a namespace on the platform. The enumerated repo list of each namespace is an input of the function: the generator records the sha256 of the sorted list as `:enumeration-sha256` in `(generation ...)`, and "the same inputs" includes that list. Two runs that observed different repo sets therefore have different inputs, and a replay uses the recorded list. `cadastra` supplies observed fields. That belongs to Stage B step 1 [R].

=== 8.2 Record

----
(estate-atlas-deed
  :schema-version "1.0.0"
  :canonical-name "<federation>-atlas"
  :beholding-chora <root-uuid>
  :registry-version "<generation counter>"      ; integer as string
  (generation :tool "<name>" :tool-version "<semver>"
              :root-hash "sha256:<64 hex>" :vocabulary-version "<semver>"
              :enumeration-sha256 "<64 hex>")
  (estate :uuid <estate-uuid> :name "<owner>" :state <state>
          :chora-hash "sha256:<64 hex>"
    (repo :uuid <repo-uuid> :name "<owner>/<name>" :state <state>
          :path "<repo>_chora.deed" :chora-hash "sha256:<64 hex>"
      (co-estate :estate <estate-uuid>)
      (finding-aid :clade <symbol>))
    (repo :name "<owner>/<name>" :state absent)))
----

A full sample is in `docs/estate-reconciliation/examples/a1-ATLAS.deed.example`. It lints clean as written: `deed.abnf` v1.3.0 and its linter accept the opaque literal since #1473 [M: linted 2026-11-12].

States, for an estate or repo record:

[cols="1,4"]
|===
| State | Condition

| `resolved` | The chora parses, its UUID checks (section 9), and `:beholding-chora` resolves to an estate chora in the federation. For an estate chora, the reference resolves to the root and the root's `(estates)` clause lists it. A repo needs no reverse listing, because no clause lists an estate's primary repos.
| `absent` | The repo exists in a namespace and has no chora. It has no UUID in the record. [R: the plan counts 8 of 15 measured repos in this state.]
| `unresolved` | `:beholding-chora` matches no estate chora in the federation.
| `legacy` | Withdrawn by Q2 (7.1). `:beholding-chora` carried the old literal `#u5"estate/chora"`. It is now an error in every mode, so no chora is indexed in this state.
| `conflict` | Two choras carry the same UUID, or one repo is listed under 2 primary estates.
| `orphan` | An estate chora beholds the root but the root's `(estates)` does not list it.
| `unconfirmed` | A co-estate edge that the other side does not confirm (section 6).
|===

=== 8.3 Hashes and determinism

* `:chora-hash` and `:root-hash` are sha256 over the file bytes after normalising CRLF to LF. Comments and whitespace are part of the bytes. A whitespace edit therefore changes the hash. A canonical form that ignores them is deferred to A2 (decision C5).
* Two runs on the same inputs produce the same bytes outside the `(generation ...)` clause and outside `:registry-version`. The records are ordered by UUID, then by name.
* `:registry-version` increases by 1 on any run whose output differs from the previous run's output outside `(generation ...)` and outside `:registry-version`. Both outputs are compared under that same exclusion.

== 9. Identity values

. *Minting* [O: U2, U3, V8]. A repo's id is minted once, from its birth path `github.com/<birth owner>/<name>`. The registry mints 2 ids from that path: `uuid`, the UUIDv5 in the URL namespace, and `uuid_v8`, the ADR-008 profile C UUIDv8 of `<domain>:github.com/<birth owner>/<name>` (first 16 bytes of SHA-256, version 8, variant 10) [M: `gv-clade-index` ADR 0008]. The domain is `chora-registry` for ids minted after V9 and `gv-clade-index` for ids minted before it; the registry archive records which domain minted each id (V9). A deed carries `uuid_v8` (rule V8). The registry archive (`gv-clade-index` `worker/data/archive.json`) holds both as generation 1 [I: I did not read `archive.json`]. I checked the profile C recipe against the oracle in `tools/uuid-v8` and against the `standards` deed after #1475: both give `b32d6be4-73ba-873e-9d61-e57f43c75cd1` for `github.com/hyperpolymath/standards` [M].
. *Carried opaquely* [O: U4]. A deed carries the id as an opaque literal, `#uuid"<8-4-4-4-12 lowercase hex>"` (section 7.1). A validator never re-derives it from the current owner and name, so a transfer, rename or move does not change it [O: U1]. The birth path appears in no deed. It stays only in the registry archive, which is not served.
. *No `:minted-name`.* The registry archive record (birth path, generation 1) replaces the `:minted-name` field that this section used to define. `:minted-name` is withdrawn, and a deed that carries it is reported. Because no retired name has to appear in a deed, decisions C4 and C6 no longer pull against each other.
. *Checking.* A validator checks the literal's shape, version and variant (section 7.1). Where it can read the registry archive, it checks that `:repo-uuid` equals the generation-1 id of the repo's archive record (T33). An id cannot be checked from the deed alone, and no check may claim otherwise.
. *Ids that are not repos.* Estate, platform and federation ids are also minted once and carried opaquely. Their preimages are `chora:estate/<host>/<owner>`, `chora:platform/<host>` and `chora:federation/<name>`, minted as profile C UUIDv8: the preimage is already `domain:name` with domain `chora`, as `#u8` bodies are (C9 [O], amended to version 8 by rule V8). The domain `chora` is registered in `docs/decisions/ADR-008-profile-c-domains.json`, with chora-registry as the minting repo, because the index that lists these ids lives there (Q3 [O], 2026-11-14; it replaces the owner choice of 2026-11-12, `metadatastician/git-logistics-office`). The register names the registry by its current GitHub name, `hyperpolymath/gv-clade-index`, until the rename lands. `scripts/check-uuid-v8-domains.sh chora` passes [M].
. Legacy value. `#u5"estate/chora"` was a legacy beholding value, accepted in `lax` mode. Under Q2 (7.1) it is an error in every mode. Each repo replaces it with its estate's opaque id, one PR per repo. That is not a sweep, and A1 does not perform it.
. A UUID proves reproducibility from the registry archive, not authenticity. This keeps the spec's own statement that `enforce-uuid-provenance` is not an unfalsifiability guarantee [M: DEED-GRAMMAR-SPEC].

== 10. K9 and estate invariants: the meet

[O] The effective invariants of a repo are the meet of the federation root's rules, its estate's rules and its K9 rules.

. Prohibitions accumulate. A K9 rule may add a prohibition. No K9 or estate rule may remove a prohibition that a higher level declares.
. A rule that permits what a higher rule forbids is a conflict. A validator reports it as an error and does not choose a side.
. The meet can be computed only for rules that carry a machine-readable effect. Today a rule is `(rule :priority N :text "...")` with prose text, so a validator can compare only equal rule identifiers. A1 reserves `:forbids` (a list of action symbols) on `rule` for the computation. The vocabulary of action symbols belongs to A2 and A5 (C3).
. K9 invariants SHOULD NOT repeat estate invariants (kept from the draft).

== 11. The clade

Amended by owner ruling W10 of 2026-11-13 (given in the A7 thread; A7 2.3), which reverses R4's "the clade is an outward finding aid only".

* *Placement is identity* [O: A7 W10]. A repo's `(clade ...)` clause records where the repo sits in the estate's classification schemes. These are identity facts, like `(lineage)`. They are never the repo's id (that is `:repo-uuid`, section 9), and no edict hangs off them (A7 W1).
* *The registry is authoritative* [O: A7 W10]. chora-registry, the renamed `gv-clade-index`, holds the estate-wide classification schemes, which can be swapped (A7 2.3). A placement carried in a chora is a copy and is checked against the registry; on a disagreement the registry wins and the chora is reported.
* *Content organisation is not identity.* How a repo organises its own content is `CLADE.deed`, a descriptile (A7 W10). It is not part of the chora.
* *Nothing resolves identity through a clade.* No check MAY derive `:repo-uuid` or any other identity field from a clade. The index generator copies placements into `(finding-aid :clade ...)` (8.2) for outward discovery, and the `boj-server` cartridges and `proven-tests-and-benches` read the index, not the chora [O].
* *New ids follow the rename* [O: V9]. New mints use the domain `chora-registry`; ids minted before V9 keep the `gv-clade-index` domain and are never reissued (section 9.1, V9).
